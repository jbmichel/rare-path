# Therapeutic development dossier: Tuberous sclerosis complex

*Decision-support output from an automated multi-agent analysis. Claims are labelled EVIDENCE / INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS. Not medical advice; replaces no experimental validation.*

## 1. Disease mechanism

**Causal defect:** Heterozygous loss-of-function variants in TSC1 (hamartin) or TSC2 (tuberin), with a somatic second hit in lesions. These are tumor-suppressor genes. TSC2 cases are generally more severe than TSC1 cases, and about 10–15% of patients have no mutation identified.

**Molecular consequence:** Loss of the TSC1/TSC2/TBC1D7 complex removes the GAP activity of TSC2 toward RHEB. RHEB then stays GTP-bound and mTORC1 is constitutively active. This drives cell growth, protein synthesis and anabolic metabolism, suppresses autophagy, and disturbs neuronal migration, synaptic function and excitability.

**Key cell types:** Neural progenitors / radial glia giving rise to dysmorphic neurons and giant cells (cortical tubers), Subependymal cells (SEN/SEGA), Perivascular epithelioid cells (PEComa: renal angiomyolipoma, LAM), Cardiac myocytes (rhabdomyoma), Skin fibroblasts (angiofibroma)

**Key pathology:**
- Cortical tubers and epileptogenic networks causing early-onset, drug-resistant epilepsy, infantile spasms and TAND (autism, intellectual disability)
- SEGA growth with risk of hydrocephalus
- Renal angiomyolipoma with bleeding risk and loss of kidney function
- Lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM), mainly in women
- Cardiac rhabdomyomas and facial angiofibromas

**Evidence strength:** Strong. The causal genes and the mTORC1 mechanism are established, and pharmacological mTORC1 inhibition improves SEGA, angiomyolipoma and seizures in randomized phase 3 trials (EXIST-1/2/3). This is direct human proof that the mechanism drives disease.

**Major unknowns:**
- Whether mTORC1-independent effects of TSC loss (for example on mTORC2, autophagy or metabolism) drive epilepsy and TAND
- How much of the neurological disease can be reversed after the developmental period, and the best timing for preventive treatment
- Why lesions regrow after rapalogs are stopped, and the incomplete seizure response (about 30–40% responders)
- Mechanism in patients with no mutation identified (mosaicism, deep intronic variants)
- Whether tissue-selective or brain-penetrant mTORC1 inhibitors could improve the therapeutic window

TSC is caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants in TSC1 or TSC2 with a somatic second hit in lesions (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:8824721). Loss of TSC2 GAP activity leaves RHEB GTP-bound and mTORC1 constitutively active, driving growth, translation, autophagy suppression and disturbed neuronal migration/excitability. The mechanism is validated in humans: rapalogs shrink SEGA and angiomyolipoma (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:24729041; NCT00789828) and reduce refractory seizures (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:30169322). The unresolved part is neurological. Real-world everolimus seizure response is ~31% (46% children vs 14% adults) (EVIDENCE, inconclusive, PMID:38042867), and EEG abnormality at ~42 weeks postmenstrual age already predicts 24-month cognitive and ASD outcome (EVIDENCE, PMID:33178126) — consistent with a substantial prenatally patterned, mTOR-inhibitor-unreachable component. Whether residual seizures reflect insufficient brain target engagement or fixed circuit pathology is the single pivotal uncertainty (HYPOTHESIS, not_studied). Lesion regrowth after rapalog withdrawal shows the action is cytostatic/suppressive (INFERENCE, inconclusive, PMID:24729041).

- **EVIDENCE** (supportive): In the randomized phase 3 trial EXIST-1, the mTORC1 inhibitor everolimus shrank SEGA, and in the subgroup with angiomyolipomas it also produced angiomyolipoma responses. [PMID:24729041; NCT00789828]
- **EVIDENCE** (supportive): In EXIST-3, adjunctive everolimus reduced treatment-refractory seizures in TSC compared with placebo. [PMID:30169322]
- **EVIDENCE** (inconclusive): In real-world use, about 31% of patients had a seizure response to everolimus, with more responders among children (46%) than adults (14%). This points to a developmental window and only partial reversibility of the neurological disease. [PMID:38042867]
- **EVIDENCE** (supportive): TSC hamartomas show loss of heterozygosity at TSC1 or TSC2, consistent with a two-hit tumor-suppressor mechanism. LOH was more frequent at TSC2. [PMID:8824721]
- **EVIDENCE** (supportive): mTOR dysregulation is the shared mechanism behind epileptogenic malformations of cortical development, including TSC tubers. [PMID:29338461]
- **HYPOTHESIS** (not_studied): Neurological features, especially TAND and epilepsy that has already become established, are partly fixed developmental consequences. Treatment that starts before seizure onset is therefore likely to give more benefit than mTOR inhibition alone can achieve later.

## 2. Therapeutic leverage points

- Depth of brain mTOR target engagement: rapamycin-resistant 4E-BP1 phosphorylation in cortex is a measurable, drug-addressable node that everolimus does not normalize; brain-penetrant catalytic inhibitors normalize it and reduce seizures in Tsc models (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:37399505; PMID:37545094).
- Timing rather than molecule: the presymptomatic window is objectively detectable — epileptiform discharges preceded seizures in 54/94 seizure-naive TSC infants under monthly video-EEG (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:33180985), and first-EEG timing/features predict 24-month outcome (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:33778971).
- Age-dependent reversibility: the 46% vs 14% child/adult everolimus response gap implies young patients retain signalling-dependent, therefore drug-addressable, disease (INFERENCE, inconclusive, PMID:38042867).
- An untested natural experiment already exists: infants dosed with sirolimus/everolimus in the first months of life for cardiac rhabdomyoma while still seizure-naive — a causal-pathway presymptomatic exposure readable retrospectively (INFERENCE, supportive).
- Therapeutic-window engineering (pulsed catalytic dosing, or low-dose rapalog plus intermittent catalytic) as the only realistic way to exploit deeper inhibition in children (HYPOTHESIS, not_studied).

### Comparative judgment

These three approaches fail in different places, and the ranking follows where the failure sits rather than how good the mechanism sounds.

Presymptomatic intervention is the only approach whose binding constraint is trial design rather than pharmacology or delivery. The agents are approved with paediatric dosing and proven CNS engagement, the window is objectively detectable by EEG (PMID:33180985), the endpoints are validated and already collected (PMID:33778971; PMID:33178126), and decisively the gating step is a retrospective cohort requiring no new infant dosing. The causal-pathway arm, presymptomatic mTOR inhibition, has never been tested while the GABAergic arm has already been falsified for cognition (PMID:37638552; PMID:41061327). That combination of untested causal rationale plus near-zero-cost first step earns PURSUE.

Catalytic mTOR inhibition has the strongest mechanistic logic and real in vivo seizure data (PMID:37545094), but its dominant failure mode — chronic TORC1/2 tolerability in children — is completely untested, and there is no de-risked chemical starting point (PMID:37399505). It sits at INVESTIGATE rather than PURSUE precisely because a compelling mechanism must not outrank an untested window and absent chemistry. The proposed experiment tests efficacy, not the failure mode; fund it only bundled with the chronic juvenile tolerability and pulsed/combination arms.

On the two modalities for the downstream axis, the same hypothesis splits cleanly. The small-molecule version is testable now with off-the-shelf compounds in a well-designed factorial add-on and gets LOW PRIORITY; the protein version is REJECTed because the arm with the better biology (IL-1, astrocyte-localized) has no parenchymal delivery path and the arm with the delivery (luminal VEGF) has the weaker story and an unacceptable paediatric chronic-dosing profile. The advantages sit in different arms and do not combine. Both share one upstream liability: the markers are reversed by mTORC1 inhibition (PMID:26003087), the signature of an epiphenomenon. The cheap route is to run the ICV protein cohort as a rider on the small-molecule add-on study and settle both at once.

What would change my mind. On prevention: a null retrospective signal, or benefit disappearing after adjustment for TSC2 status and tuber burden, would stop another presymptomatic rapalog trial outright — chronic everolimus immunosuppression in asymptomatic infants cannot be justified on hope. On catalytic inhibition: demonstrated normalization of cortical p4E-BP1 producing seizure freedom in everolimus-refractory animals together with a tolerable pulsed regimen in juvenile mice would move it to PURSUE; conversely, benefit only at exposures abolishing systemic pAKT-S473 would kill it. On the downstream axis: incremental seizure reduction surviving a near-maximal rapamycin ceiling arm with documented brain target engagement would promote the small-molecule arm to INVESTIGATE, and would be the only result justifying revisiting the protein arm's delivery engineering.

### CNS-optimized catalytic mTOR inhibition - small_molecule - INVESTIGATE

*Hypothesis:* Rapalogs only partially inhibit mTORC1 and penetrate the brain poorly, which explains the ~30-40% seizure response. A brain-penetrant ATP-competitive mTOR kinase inhibitor would suppress both rapamycin-resistant mTORC1 outputs (4E-BP/cap-dependent translation) and mTORC2 in neurons and glia, giving deeper target engagement in tubers and epileptogenic cortex.

*Target / process:* mTOR kinase (mTORC1 + mTORC2) in CNS; *desired perturbation:* Reduce hyperactive mTOR catalytic signalling in brain tissue to near-normal levels, with sustained CNS exposure at tolerated systemic doses

*Strategy:* Deliver deeper, sustained mTOR kinase inhibition in brain than rapalogs achieve, using an ATP-competitive (TORC1/2) inhibitor with CNS exposure. Practical sequencing: (1) benchmark CNS-penetrant tool compounds (TC1/related) head-to-head against everolimus in Tsc1-GFAP CKO and Tsc2 hypomorph seizure models with brain pS6/p4E-BP1/pAKT-S473 PD and free-brain-fraction PK; (2) in parallel, test whether any clinical-stage ATP-competitive mTOR inhibitor can be repurposed at a paediatric-tolerable chronic dose — none was designed for CNS, so the honest expectation is that new chemistry (brain-penetrant, non-genotoxic, P-gp-sparing) is required; (3) consider preserving the rapalog tolerability profile by combining a low-dose rapalog with intermittent/pulsed catalytic dosing rather than chronic full TORC1/2 blockade.

*Verdict rationale:* The proposed head-to-head is a genuine kill experiment: it pre-registers a pharmacodynamic acceptance criterion (cortical p4E-BP1 indistinguishable from wild-type) so a negative efficacy result is interpretable rather than a dosing failure, pairs EEG with free unbound brain concentration, and doses after seizure onset — the clinically relevant hard case. It gates a multi-year discovery programme against a cheap mouse study. Two reservations keep this at INVESTIGATE rather than PURSUE. First, it answers the efficacy premise but not the dominant failure mode; I would require the chronic juvenile tolerability arm and the pulsed/combination arm funded in the same package, since a positive efficacy result with an uncloseable window is still a dead programme. Second, germline biallelic mouse models carry more uniform and severe mTOR activation than the mosaic human two-hit lesion, and no mouse reproduces human tubers with giant cells, so deep inhibition will likely look better in mouse than in patients (INFERENCE, inconclusive). A positive result licenses a window-engineering programme, not a candidate.

**NO EXISTING AGENT IDENTIFIED**

*Delivery:* tissue Brain — cortical tubers, perituberal cortex, subependymal zone; cell Dysmorphic neurons, giant cells, astrocytes; secondarily SEGA cells; access Oral small molecule; SEGA and periventricular lesions sit at a leaky BBB/CSF interface and are already reached by everolimus (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:24729041). Diffuse cortex and perituberal tissue are the hard compartment: adequate free-brain concentration requires low P-gp efflux and high unbound fraction, which no clinical-stage ATP-competitive mTOR inhibitor was optimized for (INFERENCE, contradictory).; constraint: Not organ access but therapeutic window: mTOR is essential in all CNS and peripheral cells, so the limit is tolerated systemic exposure, not BBB crossing per se. Brain-selective PK (or intermittent dosing) is the only realistic way to widen it.

*Biggest unknown:* Whether residual seizures on everolimus reflect insufficient brain target engagement (measurable: does normalizing brain p4E-BP1 abolish seizures in a model where everolimus does not?) or fixed circuit pathology. Until a model experiment separates these, the whole programme rests on an untested premise.

*Model fitness (partial):* Tsc2 hypomorphic mouse with spontaneous, translationally relevant seizures (PMID:37545094), cross-checked in Tsc1-GFAP conditional knockout and neuronal Tsc1-KO mice (PMID:37399505). Mechanism: Yes for the core mechanism: biallelic Tsc loss drives constitutive mTORC1 with elevated pS6 and p4E-BP1 in brain, and the readouts needed (regional p4E-BP1 as the rapamycin-resistant output, pAKT-S473 as the mTORC2 output) are directly measurable in the same tissue that generates the seizures. EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:37399505; PMID:37545094. Cell type: Partial. Astrocytes, neurons and neural progenitors carrying Tsc loss are represented, and the Tsc1-GFAP CKO gives a glial-driven epilepsy phenotype. No mouse reproduces human cortical tubers with giant cells or the laminar disorganization of human perituberal cortex, so target engagement in 'tuber tissue' is modelled only by proxy. INFERENCE, inconclusive. Phenotype: Continuous video-EEG seizure frequency and burden, plus survival, with paired pharmacodynamics (cortical and perituberal-equivalent pS6, p4E-BP1, pAKT-S473) and free (unbound) brain and plasma drug concentrations. This is the exact pairing the decision needs: did we normalize the rapamycin-resistant output, and did seizures follow? Limits: Mouse seizures do not predict human seizure response quantitatively; germline biallelic models have more uniform and more severe mTOR activation than the mosaic two-hit human lesion, which may make deep inhibition look better than it will be in patients. Mouse P-gp handling differs from human, so free-brain-fraction translation is approximate. No model addresses chronic paediatric tolerability of TORC1/2 blockade, which is the programme's real failure mode. The available catalytic compounds are tool compounds with genotoxicity and primate tolerability liabilities, so a positive result licenses a discovery programme, not a candidate. EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:37399505.

*Best next experiment:* Head-to-head, pharmacodynamically anchored seizure study. In the Tsc2 hypomorphic spontaneous-seizure model (PMID:37545094), with the Tsc1-GFAP CKO as a second genotype, run three arms plus vehicle: (1) everolimus at a maximal chronically tolerated regimen; (2) a brain-penetrant ATP-competitive mTOR tool compound (TC1 or congener, PMID:37399505; PMID:37545094) dose-titrated in a pilot PK/PD cohort to the lowest dose that normalizes cortical p4E-BP1 to wild-type levels; (3) everolimus plus intermittent/pulsed catalytic dosing, to test whether mTORC2 sparing can be preserved. Treat from after seizure onset (the clinically relevant, hardest case). Primary: continuous video-EEG for 3-4 weeks (baseline and on-treatment). In parallel terminal cohorts at matched timepoints: regional immunoblot/immunohistochemistry for pS6-S235/236, p4E-BP1-T37/46 and pAKT-S473 in cortex, hippocampus and subependymal zone, with simultaneous unbound brain and plasma concentrations (brain homogenate binding / equilibrium dialysis). Power the EEG arms for a 50 percent between-arm difference in seizure frequency. Pre-register the PD acceptance criterion: an arm only counts as 'deep engagement achieved' if cortical p4E-BP1 is statistically indistinguishable from wild-type. (model: Tsc2 hypomorphic mouse with spontaneous seizures (PMID:37545094), confirmed in Tsc1-GFAP conditional knockout; treatment initiated after seizure onset.; readout: Percent reduction in video-EEG seizure frequency and total seizure burden versus vehicle, interpreted strictly against the paired pharmacodynamic readout - cortical p4E-BP1 normalization - and free-brain drug concentration. Secondary: pAKT-S473 suppression as the mTORC2 toxicity surrogate, survival, and body weight/growth as a first tolerability signal.).

- Question: Do residual seizures on maximal allosteric mTORC1 inhibition reflect insufficient brain target engagement (specifically, persistent rapamycin-resistant 4E-BP1 phosphorylation), or fixed circuit pathology that deeper mTOR kinase inhibition cannot reach? Everything downstream - whether to fund multi-year CNS-optimized chemistry - depends on this single answer.
- Supports if: The catalytic arm normalizes cortical p4E-BP1 where everolimus does not, and this is accompanied by a substantially larger seizure reduction (for example seizure freedom or greater than 75 percent reduction versus roughly 30-40 percent for everolimus) in animals that are everolimus-refractory. This establishes that residual seizures are target-engagement-limited and that the differentiating pharmacology is real. Decision: proceed, but immediately to the window question, not to chemistry - run the chronic juvenile tolerability study of the pulsed/combination arm before committing to lead optimization.
- Weakens/kills if: Full normalization of cortical p4E-BP1 (confirmed by PD, not assumed from dose) produces no meaningful additional seizure reduction over maximally dosed everolimus, or improves seizures only at exposures that also abolish pAKT-S473 systemically and cause weight loss/overt toxicity. Either outcome refutes the premise: the first says the residual disease is fixed circuit pathology, the second says the mechanism cannot be separated from its toxicity. Decision: do not fund CNS-optimized catalytic mTOR chemistry for epilepsy; redirect to earlier/preventive treatment timing (pre-seizure-onset dosing, the stronger hypothesis given the 46 percent paediatric versus 14 percent adult response, PMID:38042867) or to mTOR-independent circuit targets.
- Decision enabled: Whether to commit to a multi-year, de novo CNS-optimized ATP-competitive mTOR inhibitor discovery programme. There is currently no de-risked chemical starting point - published brain-penetrant catalytic compounds carry in vitro genotoxicity, poor oral exposure in higher species and dose-limiting primate toxicity, and no clinical-stage agent was CNS-optimized (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:37399505) - so the cost of being wrong is very high and the cost of this experiment is low. A negative result stops the programme for roughly the price of one mouse EEG study; a positive result redefines the programme as a therapeutic-window problem, where the pulsed/combination arm is the de-risking strategy rather than chronic full TORC1/2 blockade.

### Presymptomatic window: EEG-guided prevention - small_molecule - PURSUE

*Hypothesis:* Much of TSC neurological disability is the consequence of early, often subclinical epileptiform activity and spasms acting on a developing cortex. Intervening before clinical seizure onset — triggered by EEG epileptiform discharges in genetically diagnosed infants — modifies epileptogenesis rather than treating established disease. The same logic argues for testing mTOR inhibition (not only GABAergic drugs) inside this window.

*Target / process:* Epileptogenesis in infancy; GABA transaminase (vigabatrin) and/or early mTORC1 inhibition during the critical developmental period; *desired perturbation:* Suppress epileptiform network activity and mTORC1 hyperactivation before seizure onset, to prevent infantile spasms, drug-resistant epilepsy and secondary developmental injury

*Strategy:* Use EEG-triggered presymptomatic dosing in genetically diagnosed TSC infants. Agents are already approved: vigabatrin (GABA-transaminase inhibitor) for spasm/epilepsy prevention, and everolimus/sirolimus to act on the causal driver during the same window. Given PREVeNT, the defensible design is no longer 'vigabatrin prevents disability' but (a) vigabatrin at first epileptiform EEG for the epilepsy/spasm endpoint only, and (b) a separate test of early mTOR inhibition with neurodevelopmental endpoints, where the causal rationale is distinct from GABAergic seizure suppression.

*Verdict rationale:* This is the only approach where the experiment is both cheap and decision-complete, and where the proposer learned from the field's failure rather than restating its hopes. Step 1 — retrospectively assembling seizure-naive TSC infants incidentally exposed to sirolimus/everolimus for cardiac rhabdomyoma, matched on genotype, tuber burden and first-EEG phenotype, scored blind — exploits an existing human natural experiment, requires no new infant dosing, and tests the causal-pathway arm before anyone spends a cohort on it. Confounding by rhabdomyoma severity is real but manageable by matching, an acceptable price for a signal that gates a trial (INFERENCE, inconclusive). Step 2's design fix is the substantive contribution: permitting open-label vigabatrin rescue in both arms so the randomized contrast is mTOR inhibition alone structurally removes the crossover that destroyed PREVeNT (INFERENCE, PMID:37638552). I explicitly endorse powering on seizure/EEG endpoints with cognition secondary — given PREVeNT and the predictive first EEG, powering on cognition is the most likely way to waste the only realistic infant prevention cohort. PURSUE is earned by human causal evidence, approved agents, validated endpoints and a cheap gating step. The honest caveat is that the ceiling may be epilepsy trajectory only; that is still worth having, and Step 1 will say so before anyone is dosed.

*Delivery:* tissue Developing infant cortex; cell Neurons/interneurons (vigabatrin, via GABA-T in neurons and astrocytes) and tuber cells/astrocytes/progenitors (mTOR inhibitors); access Vigabatrin is CNS-penetrant and orally dosed in infants as standard of care (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:37638552). Everolimus reaches SEGA and reduces seizures in children, so functionally relevant CNS engagement is proven, though diffuse cortical exposure is likely submaximal (EVIDENCE/INFERENCE, PMID:30169322).; constraint: Not molecular delivery but the clinical window: identifying infants early enough requires genetic/prenatal diagnosis plus serial video-EEG surveillance infrastructure, and treating children who may never become symptomatic forces a hard risk-benefit (vigabatrin retinal toxicity; chronic everolimus immunosuppression in infancy).

*Biggest unknown:* Whether any postnatal intervention can move cognition/ASD, i.e. how much TAND is prenatally fixed. PREVeNT is the first serious negative answer for a GABAergic drug; whether a causal-pathway drug (mTOR inhibitor) started at first epileptiform EEG does better is genuinely untested and is the decision-relevant question.

*Model fitness (partial):* Human: prospectively followed seizure-naive TSC infant cohorts with serial video-EEG and 24-month standardized outcomes (EPISTOP design, PMID:33180985; EEG-outcome mapping PMID:33778971, PMID:33178126) — plus the natural experiment of infants given sirolimus/everolimus in the first months of life for haemodynamically significant cardiac rhabdomyoma, i.e. for a non-neurological indication, before any seizure.. Mechanism: Yes for the human question actually being asked. The causal defect, the second-hit lesions and the developmental timing are all native to the human infant; no model system reproduces a prenatally patterned human cortex plus a 24-month cognitive/ASD readout (EVIDENCE that EEG at ~42 weeks postmenstrual age already predicts 24-month ASD risk and developmental quotients, PMID:33178126 — meaning the relevant biology is in place before any postnatal drug can act). Cell type: Yes — perituberal cortex, dysmorphic neurons, astrocytes and progenitors in situ, in the actual developmental window. Both candidate agents already have paediatric dosing and proven functional CNS engagement in children (everolimus seizure reduction, PMID:30169322; vigabatrin standard-of-care infant dosing, PMID:37638552) (EVIDENCE, supportive). ### Downstream damage: neurovascular/neuroinflammatory axis - protein - REJECT

*Hypothesis:* Beyond cell growth, TSC loss drives VEGF overexpression, abnormal cerebral vasculature with blood-brain-barrier leak, and astrocyte-derived IL-1b/CXCL10 inflammation. These are proximate contributors to seizures and ongoing injury, and blocking them could reduce seizure burden without needing full mTOR suppression — and could complement, or substitute where, mTOR inhibition is not tolerated.

*Target / process:* VEGF/VEGFR signalling, BBB integrity, and IL-1b/astrocytic neuroinflammation in TSC brain; *desired perturbation:* Restore BBB integrity and lower neuroinflammatory signalling (VEGFR antagonism, TGF-b/ALK5 modulation, IL-1 pathway blockade)

*Strategy:* Two separable biologic arms. *Verdict rationale:* I accept the proposed ICV experiment as scientifically well-posed while rejecting the programme, and these are not in tension. That makes it a true ceiling test — if saturating CNS IL-1Ra adds nothing to rapamycin, no shuttle engineering can rescue the arm and the node is killed permanently. *Biggest unknown:* Whether these changes contribute causally and mTOR-independently to seizures. This is decidable in one well-powered add-on experiment (rapamycin alone versus rapamycin + apatinib/IL-1 blockade versus vehicle, with seizure burden by continuous EEG), and that experiment should gate any further investment.

*Model fitness (partial):* Tsc1-GFAP conditional knockout mouse (Tsc1^flox/flox; GFAP-Cre), with continuous video-EEG seizure quantification; the same model in which astrocytic IL-1b/CXCL10 elevation (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:26003087) and elevated brain VEGF with two-photon-confirmed BBB leak plus apatinib/RepSox intervention (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:38049961) were demonstrated.. Mechanism: Yes for the specific question asked. It reproduces Tsc1 loss, mTORC1 hyperactivation, spontaneous recurrent seizures, astrocytic IL-1b/CXCL10 induction and BBB leak, and it is rapamycin-responsive — so it can support a factorial rapamycin x anti-axis design (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:26003087; PMID:38049961). It does not model the two-hit somatic architecture or human cortical tubers (INFERENCE, inconclusive). Cell type: Partially. Astrocytes are the driven cell type and are the putative IL-1b source (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:26003087), and brain microvascular endothelium shows the VEGF/BBB phenotype (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:38049961). But GFAP-Cre deletion is astrocyte-biased, whereas human epileptogenesis involves dysmorphic neurons and giant cells arising from radial glia; the neuronal-lesional compartment is under-represented (INFERENCE, contradictory). Phenotype: Spontaneous seizure frequency and total seizure burden by continuous video-EEG; survival; plus mechanistic readouts (cortical/hippocampal IL-1b and CXCL10, phospho-S6, BBB permeability by tracer extravasation or two-photon imaging) (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:26003087; PMID:38049961). Limits: Single-model dependence — the entire hypothesis rests on this one conditional knockout (INFERENCE, inconclusive, PMID:38049961). Rapamycin is near-curative for seizures in this model, which compresses the ceiling for detecting incremental add-on benefit and may force a submaximal-rapamycin reference arm. Apatinib is a P-gp substrate with limited CNS exposure and RepSox is a non-clinical tool compound, so mouse-level target engagement does not read through to a translatable molecule (INFERENCE, contradictory, PMID:38049961). Astrocyte-restricted Cre over-weights the glial contribution.

*Best next experiment:* Factorial add-on study in Tsc1-GFAP CKO mice (n~12-15/arm, both sexes, randomised, blinded scoring), 6 arms: (1) vehicle; (2) submaximal rapamycin (a dose titrated to ~50% seizure-burden reduction, deliberately leaving headroom to detect addition); (3) submaximal rapamycin + apatinib (VEGFR2); (4) submaximal rapamycin + IL-1 axis blockade (IL-1R antagonism or a caspase-1 inhibitor as the CNS-oriented small-molecule option); (5) near-maximal rapamycin alone as the efficacy ceiling; (6) apatinib or IL-1 blockade as monotherapy (secondary, for interpretation only). Treat from P21 (post-symptom-onset) with continuous video-EEG from P25 to endpoint. Mandatory pharmacodynamic gate measured in the same animals: brain-tissue drug concentration and target engagement (cortical phospho-VEGFR2 for apatinib; cortical mature IL-1b for the IL-1 arm) — a negative efficacy result is uninterpretable without proof the target was engaged in brain. (model: Tsc1^flox/flox;GFAP-Cre mouse with continuous video-EEG; the published model for both the inflammatory and neurovascular phenotypes (PMID:26003087; PMID:38049961).; readout: Total seizure burden (seconds of electrographic seizure per 24 h) over the recording window, compared between submaximal-rapamycin alone and submaximal-rapamycin + add-on. Secondary: seizure frequency, survival, cortical IL-1b/CXCL10, BBB tracer leak, phospho-S6.).

- Question: Does blocking the VEGFR/BBB or IL-1 axis reduce seizure burden ON TOP of mTORC1 inhibition — i.e. is any part of this axis an mTOR-independent causal driver rather than a reversible downstream readout of mTORC1 hyperactivation?
- Supports if: Add-on arm shows a clear, pre-specified incremental reduction in seizure burden over submaximal rapamycin alone (e.g. >=40% further reduction, p<0.05) WITH documented brain target engagement, and ideally residual benefit even against near-maximal rapamycin. This would establish an mTOR-independent causal contribution and justify moving to the agent problem — specifically a medicinal-chemistry effort for a CNS-penetrant, chronically paediatric-dosable IL-1 axis inhibitor, since neither apatinib nor RepSox is a viable clinical molecule.
- Weakens/kills if: No incremental seizure-burden reduction over submaximal rapamycin despite confirmed brain target engagement and confirmed suppression of the axis biomarker (IL-1b/CXCL10 or BBB leak). This is the expected outcome given that mTORC1 inhibition alone already reverses these markers (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:26003087), and it would mean the axis is an epiphenomenon.
- Decision enabled: Go/no-go on the entire neurovascular-neuroinflammatory programme in TSC, for a few hundred thousand dollars and ~6-9 months. Negative => stop; redirect resources to brain-penetrant/tissue-selective mTOR inhibition and to earlier/preventive dosing, which is where the unmet need actually sits. Positive => fund de novo CNS-penetrant chemistry on the IL-1 node and only then consider clinical translation; do NOT advance apatinib or any chronic systemic anti-angiogenic into a paediatric lifelong indication regardless of result (INFERENCE, contradictory — hypertension, growth-plate and wound-healing toxicity, on top of existing angiomyolipoma bleeding risk). HYPOTHESIS (not_studied): a partial-rapamycin-sparing combination could widen the therapeutic window; this experiment is the only cheap test of it.

## 4. What is already known

- Human randomized proof of mechanism exists: everolimus shrinks SEGA and angiomyolipoma (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:24729041; NCT00789828) and reduces treatment-refractory seizures (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:30169322).
- Efficacy on the neurological axis is partial and age-dependent: ~31% real-world seizure response, 46% in children vs 14% in adults (EVIDENCE, inconclusive, PMID:38042867).
- A clearly failed approach: PREVeNT, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled vigabatrin at first epileptiform EEG, showed no Bayley-III cognitive benefit at 24 months and no reduction in focal seizures (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:37638552), with no prevention of intellectual disability or ASD at 36 months (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:41061327).
- Both prevention trials are compromised as prevention tests: PREVeNT lost exposure contrast when 19/27 placebo infants crossed to open-label vigabatrin at median 44 days (EVIDENCE, inconclusive, PMID:37638552); EPISTOP could not randomize at 4 of 10 centres (EVIDENCE, inconclusive, PMID:39625912).
- The presymptomatic window is real and detectable: epileptiform discharges preceded seizures in 54/94 seizure-naive infants (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:33180985); first-EEG timing predicts 24-month outcome (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:33778971).
- Much of the relevant biology precedes any postnatal drug: EEG at ~42 weeks postmenstrual age already predicts 24-month ASD risk and developmental quotient (EVIDENCE, PMID:33178126).
- Brain-penetrant catalytic mTOR inhibitors exist but only as tool compounds: in vitro genotoxicity, poor oral exposure in higher species, dose-limiting cynomolgus toxicity (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:37399505); TC1 nonetheless reduced seizures in a Tsc2 hypomorph (EVIDENCE, supportive, PMID:37545094).
- The ATP-competitive class is chemically mature but never produced a chronically dosable agent (vistusertib, sapanisertib, AZD8055; ChEMBL CHEMBL2842) (INFERENCE, contradictory).
- No TSC trial of a catalytic mTOR inhibitor, of presymptomatic mTOR inhibition, or of IL-1/VEGF blockade for epilepsy was identified (EVIDENCE, ClinicalTrials.gov 'Tuberous sclerosis complex'; TSC+anakinra returned 0 studies).
- Neuroinflammatory markers in Tsc1-GFAP CKO are reversed by mTORC1 inhibition — the signature of a downstream readout (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:26003087).
- Lesions regrow after rapalog withdrawal: the action is suppressive, implying lifelong therapy (INFERENCE, inconclusive, PMID:24729041).
- Vigabatrin carries irreversible retinal toxicity, now unoffset by demonstrated cognitive benefit (INFERENCE, contradictory, PMID:31912454).

## 5. Critical gaps

- Whether residual seizures on maximal rapalog reflect insufficient brain target engagement (persistent cortical p4E-BP1 phosphorylation) or fixed circuit pathology. The catalytic programme cannot be costed until this is answered.
- Whether chronic TORC1/2 inhibition at brain-effective exposure is tolerable in juveniles. This is the dominant failure mode of the catalytic approach and no model or study currently addresses it.
- Whether presymptomatic mTOR inhibition changes epilepsy trajectory in seizure-naive infants — the one arm with a causal rationale that has never been tested.
- How much TAND is prenatally fixed versus driven by postnatal epileptiform activity. PREVeNT gives the first serious negative answer for a GABAergic drug; the causal-pathway equivalent is unknown.
- Whether any part of the neurovascular/neuroinflammatory axis is mTOR-independent. All retrieved evidence is consistent with pure downstream readout.
- No model reproduces human cortical tubers with giant cells or perituberal laminar disorganization; target engagement in human tuber tissue is modelled only by proxy (INFERENCE, inconclusive).
- No de-risked chemical starting point for a CNS-optimized, non-genotoxic, P-gp-sparing catalytic mTOR inhibitor (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:37399505).

## 6. What should be done next

1. **Assemble, from TSC centres and registries, every infant who received sirolimus or everolimus in the first 6 months of life for cardiac rhabdomyoma or another non-epilepsy indication while still seizure-naive, matched to unexposed seizure-naive TSC infants on genotype (TSC2 vs TSC1), MRI tuber burden and first-EEG phenotype; score outcomes blind to exposure.**
   - Answers: Does starting an mTOR inhibitor before clinical seizure onset change the epilepsy trajectory in TSC infants?
   - Requires: Retrospective multi-centre cohort assembly and blinded chart/EEG review; 6-12 months; no new dosing.
   - Supports if: Materially later first seizure or lower drug-resistant epilepsy rate (e.g. HR <=0.6) surviving adjustment for genotype and tuber burden, ideally with a dose/timing gradient. Fund the Step 2 randomized trial.
   - Weakens/kills if: No shift in seizure timing or epilepsy severity despite months of presymptomatic exposure, or benefit vanishing on adjustment for TSC2 status and tuber burden. Do not run another presymptomatic rapalog trial.
2. **Run the pharmacodynamically anchored head-to-head seizure study in the Tsc2 hypomorph (cross-checked in Tsc1-GFAP CKO), treating after seizure onset: maximal tolerated everolimus vs a brain-penetrant catalytic tool compound titrated to normalize cortical p4E-BP1 vs everolimus plus pulsed catalytic vs vehicle, with continuous video-EEG, regional pS6/p4E-BP1/pAKT-S473 and unbound brain and plasma concentrations. Pre-register the PD acceptance criterion.**
   - Answers: Are residual seizures target-engagement-limited or circuit-fixed?
   - Requires: Pilot PK/PD dose-finding cohort then 3-4 week blinded EEG study with matched terminal PD cohorts; powered for a 50% between-arm difference.
   - Supports if: The catalytic arm normalizes cortical p4E-BP1 where everolimus does not and delivers substantially greater seizure reduction (seizure freedom or >75% vs ~30-40%) in everolimus-refractory animals. Proceed to the window question, not to chemistry.
   - Weakens/kills if: Confirmed p4E-BP1 normalization yields no additional seizure benefit, or benefit only at exposures that abolish systemic pAKT-S473 and cause weight loss/overt toxicity. Do not fund CNS-optimized catalytic chemistry for epilepsy.
3. **In the same colony, run a chronic juvenile tolerability study of the pulsed/low-dose-rapalog-plus-intermittent-catalytic regimen: growth, immune competence, metabolic parameters and neurodevelopmental behaviour over a developmentally relevant dosing period.**
   - Answers: Can deep mTOR kinase inhibition be dosed chronically in a developing animal at brain-effective exposure?
   - Requires: Juvenile chronic dosing cohort with growth, immunophenotyping, metabolic panel and behavioural battery; run in parallel with, not after, the efficacy study.
   - Supports if: An intermittent or combination regimen preserves brain p4E-BP1 suppression while sparing systemic pAKT-S473, with normal growth and immune competence. The programme becomes a tractable window-engineering problem.
   - Weakens/kills if: No regimen separates brain efficacy from systemic mTORC2 loss, growth failure or immunosuppression. The differentiating pharmacology can never be dosed in children; stop.
4. **Run the factorial add-on study in Tsc1-GFAP CKO mice against a submaximal rapamycin reference arm (titrated to ~50% seizure-burden reduction) with a near-maximal rapamycin ceiling arm, testing apatinib and IL-1 axis blockade, with brain drug concentration and target engagement as a mandatory gate; attach the chronic ICV IL-1Ra cohort as a rider.**
   - Answers: Is any part of the neurovascular/neuroinflammatory axis an mTOR-independent causal seizure driver?
   - Requires: Six-arm randomized blinded mouse EEG study, n~12-15/arm, both sexes, dosed from P21; 6-9 months; plus ICV minipump cohort with CSF exposure and cortical IL-1R engagement.
   - Supports if: Pre-specified incremental seizure-burden reduction (e.g. >=40%) over submaximal rapamycin with documented brain target engagement, ideally persisting against the near-maximal ceiling arm.
   - Weakens/kills if: No incremental benefit despite confirmed brain target engagement and confirmed suppression of the axis biomarker — including saturating ICV IL-1Ra. Closes both the small-molecule and protein arms permanently.
5. **Design, contingent on action 1, a randomized trial of everolimus added at first epileptiform EEG, powered on EEG/seizure endpoints with cognition secondary, pre-specifying open-label vigabatrin rescue permitted in BOTH arms so the randomized contrast is mTOR inhibition alone.**
   - Answers: Does presymptomatic mTOR inhibition prevent drug-resistant epilepsy, and can a prevention trial survive rescue crossover?
   - Requires: Prospective multi-centre EEG-triggered randomized trial with serial video-EEG surveillance infrastructure.
   - Supports if: Lower epileptiform EEG burden and higher proportion seizure-free at 24 months in the everolimus arm.
   - Weakens/kills if: No seizure/EEG separation despite documented exposure, confirming the trajectory is set before postnatal intervention can act.

**Do not fund yet:**
- De novo CNS-optimized ATP-competitive mTOR inhibitor chemistry (lead optimization, tox packages). There is no de-risked starting point and the premise that deeper engagement converts non-responders is untested; the mouse PD/EEG study settles this for the price of one study (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:37399505).
- Any clinical trial of a catalytic mTOR inhibitor in TSC children before a chronic juvenile tolerability study exists. The dominant failure mode is the therapeutic window and it is currently unmeasured.
- A BBB-shuttle-engineered IL-1Ra or anti-IL-1b programme. This is de novo engineering, not repurposing, and should not be entered without a positive saturating-ICV result in hand (INFERENCE, contradictory).
- Any chronic systemic anti-angiogenic (apatinib or other VEGFR TKI, bevacizumab) in a paediatric lifelong TSC indication, regardless of mouse outcome — hypertension, growth-plate and wound-healing toxicity on top of existing angiomyolipoma bleeding risk (INFERENCE, contradictory).
- Another presymptomatic vigabatrin trial powered on cognitive endpoints. PREVeNT and its 36-month follow-up have answered this (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:37638552; PMID:41061327).
- Any infant prevention trial powered primarily on cognition. Given that the first EEG already predicts 24-month outcome (PMID:33178126), this is the most likely way to waste the one realistic cohort.
- A biologic monotherapy arm in the downstream axis. The add-on-to-rapamycin design is the only one that distinguishes an independent driver from a reversible readout (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:26003087).
