## Candidates considered
| Role | Concept | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| LEAD | Variant-directed steric-block splice correction of a mapped CDKL5 splice lesion (archetype c.2376+5G>A exon-16 skipping) | Only concept with a real mapped target, an unaltered MANE coding product, one oligo, and direct human precedent for the molecular action; already rescued by engineered U1 snRNA, so an ASO is a modality upgrade rather than a discovery bet. The limitation is population size, not mechanism. |
| BACKUP | Suppress a non-productive / NMD-coupled CDKL5 splicing event (poison-exon type, e.g. ENST00000977893) | Ideal mutation-agnostic product and strongest cross-target architecture precedent, but existence is unestablished: no NMD route in the gene's definitive transcript characterisation, absent from a genome-wide AS-NMD screen, and the Ensembl NMD-biotype entry is unquantified. Gate on a flux-quantification screen before any design. |
| BACKUP | 3'UTR / alternative-polyadenylation steric stabilisation (6.6 kb exon-19 UTR, proximal AATAAA, miR-320b site) | Largest accessible coding-sequence-preserving element surface with two experimentally mapped polyA signals, but no rate-limiting element demonstrated in neurons, likely needs multiple oligos, and removing the gene's own brake fights the bounded-window requirement in a duplication-sensitive gene. |
| WATCH | Suppress constitutive intron retention in CDKL5 pre-mRNA (ENST00000623610 anchor) | No percent-intron-retention value exists for any CDKL5 intron in any human neuronal dataset, and the dominant brain isoform's terminal read-through will generate false retention calls. Same discovery screen as the poison-exon arm, so it costs nothing extra to measure but cannot be designed today. |
| WATCH | Transcriptional upregulation via a cis natural antisense transcript (antagoNAT) at the CDKL5 locus | Would give exactly the desired product and acts upstream of all splicing uncertainty, but no cis NAT at Xp22.13 was retrievable; the locus is regulated by DNA methylation across a >1 kb CpG island, which ASOs cannot address. Needs strand-specific nuclear RNA-seq/CAGE before design. |
| REJECT | Relieve 5'UTR translational repression (uORF or structured element) | Direction of effect is wrong: two algorithms predicted no significant uORF, and the human -189 C>T variant is loss-of-function, so the element is translation-promoting and masking it would lower CDKL5. Three brain leaders also converge on one ATG, forcing multiple oligos, and the mechanism changes protein per transcript, not mRNA abundance. |
| REJECT | Redirect isoform choice to full-length hCDKL5_1 via alternative last exon / 3'-end switch | The assumed direction is inverted — hCDKL5_1 is already ~90% of brain transcript and the long 20/21/22-exon form is adult-testis-specific with benign variants, so forcing it would convert the functional neuronal isoform into a neurologically irrelevant one. The only legitimate version, blocking in-frame exon 17 (~10%), adds no productive mRNA at all. |
| REJECT | Reactivate the silenced wild-type allele with an XIST ASO (± DNMT inhibitor) | Target is known and druggable but single-agent XIST knockdown is insufficient — robust reactivation required co-inhibition of DNA methylation, i.e. chronic paediatric nucleoside exposure — and it is not allele-selective, reactivating the mutant allele in half of neurons while de-repressing hundreds of X-linked genes. A locus-specific epigenome editor already achieves the goal in heterozygous female mice and patient organoids. |
| REJECT | Stabilise the mutant transcript by blocking NMD / EJC deposition | Product is a truncated kinase the brief excludes; only the latest C-terminal truncations have any human evidence of mildness and even those span mild to severe, while any PTC before residue ~297 destroys the catalytic domain, ATP site and Ser135. Also risks dominant-negative interference with wild-type protein in the other half of neurons. |
| REJECT | Exon skipping / multi-exon skipping of CDKL5 coding exons | No variant, no shared mutant transcript, no target exon, and the enumeration returned exon_skipping_applies = false. Endogenous exon-16 skipping is itself pathogenic here and pathogenic missense cluster in the catalytic domain, so internal deletion destroys function — treat induced coding-exon skipping as an off-mechanism hazard to screen against. |
| REJECT | RNase H gapmer or allele-selective knockdown of CDKL5 | Moves a haploinsufficiency disease in the wrong direction and adds no functional protein by removing a null or already-NMD-degraded transcript. Useful only as a wrong-direction control in the payload screen and as a hazard to design away from. |
| WATCH | Modulate a downstream effector instead of CDKL5 (e.g. KCC2 activation) | Abandons the restorative goal and is symptomatic, but it has the only in vivo pharmacological rescue among the non-CDKL5 options and is the rational fallback if no CDKL5 cis handle survives the discovery screen. Note three Phase 3 symptomatic agents, including positive fenfluramine data, already compete for this population. |
| WATCH | TfR1 antibody/Fab–oligonucleotide conjugate, intravenous (delivery alternative) | More uniform CNS coverage than intrathecal naked ASO in mice and macaques, splice-switching-compatible in human muscle (AOC 1044), and avoids repeat lumbar punctures — but no human CNS PD yet, new cardiac and erythroid safety surfaces, no paediatric data, and all platforms proprietary. Second-generation upgrade, not a dependency. |
| REJECT | Divalent/branched or lipid-conjugated siRNA, intrathecal (delivery alternative) | Best documented deep-brain reach and durability of any clinical CNS oligo class, but mechanism-mismatched: RNAi cannot perform splice correction, poison-exon suppression or steric 3'UTR masking. Would only apply if strategy shifted to degrading a NAT that does not appear to exist. |

## Mechanisms considered
| Mechanism | Fit | Alleles | Discovery burden | Requires |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Suppress a non-productive / NMD-coupled CDKL5 splicing event (poison-exon or similar) | possible | both | unknown whether a target exists | A real, reasonably-used non-productive splicing event in CDKL5 pre-mRNA in human neurons, with enough flux through it that diverting it raises productive mRNA meaningfully; plus verified transcript/exon annotation. |
| Suppress constitutive intron retention in CDKL5 pre-mRNA | possible | both | unknown whether a target exists | A measurable, non-trivial fraction of CDKL5 transcripts retaining a specific intron in human neurons, and that those transcripts are a recoverable reservoir rather than already-degraded; verified intron coordinates. |
| Redirect isoform choice toward full-length hCDKL5_1 (alternative last exon / 3'-end isoform switch) | possible | both | target must be located | Documented alternative-last-exon or alternative-3'-end usage in CDKL5 in the relevant human neurons, and evidence the short isoform is dispensable (not itself required). |
| Relieve 5'UTR / upstream-ORF translational repression | possible | both | unknown whether a target exists | A genuinely repressive uORF or structural element in the CDKL5 5'UTR, and resolution of which alternative first exon (1/1a/1b) supplies the 5'UTR in target neurons, since the element may exist in only one. |
| 3'UTR / alternative polyadenylation stabilisation | possible | both | unknown whether a target exists | A functional destabilising element, miRNA site, or competing polyA site in the CDKL5 3'UTR that is rate-limiting for CDKL5 abundance in neurons. |
| Transcriptional upregulation via a natural antisense transcript (antagoNAT) or other regulatory RNA at the CDKL5 locus | possible | both | unknown whether a target exists | Existence of a repressive cis-antisense or regulatory RNA at the human CDKL5 locus that is expressed in neurons and whose removal raises CDKL5; a nuclear-acting ASO. |
| Reactivate the silenced wild-type allele by targeting XIST / X-inactivation machinery | poor | depends | target known | Ongoing XIST-dependent maintenance of silencing in postnatal human neurons, and skewing that is not already favourable; a nuclear ASO reaching broad CNS. |
| Exon skipping / multi-exon skipping | not applicable | mutant only | unknown whether a target exists | A defined exonic lesion whose removal restores reading frame, plus evidence the shortened kinase retains catalytic and localisation function. |
| Exon inclusion / blocking a cryptic splice site / restoring a weakened splice site | not applicable | mutant only | target must be located | A specific splice-altering variant with a mapped cryptic site or disrupted element in that individual's allele. |
| RNase H gapmer or steric knockdown of CDKL5 transcript (including allele-selective mutant knockdown) | not applicable | depends | target known | For allele selectivity, a mutant-specific sequence or phased heterozygous SNP; for any version, that lowering CDKL5 or its mutant product is beneficial. |
| Stabilise the mutant transcript by blocking NMD or exon-junction-complex deposition | poor | mutant only | target must be located | A variant that generates a PTC in a defined position, and a truncated protein with residual therapeutic activity. |
| Modulate a downstream effector or a repressor of CDKL5 pathway output (non-CDKL5 target) | poor | normal only | unknown whether a target exists | A validated, druggable downstream node whose modulation corrects CDD-relevant neuronal phenotypes, with human genetic or pharmacological support. |

## Products
**Suppress a non-productive / NMD-coupled CDKL5 splicing event (poison-exon-type), e.g. the event generating ENST00000977893** (plausible) — The product is exactly the unaltered brain isoform and the approach is mutation-agnostic, but no CDKL5 poison exon with meaningful neuronal flux has been described, so this is a discovery project whose feasibility rests on an unquantified NMD-biotype annotation.  
Natural: Closest human equivalent is not a deletion but favourable X-inactivation: GeneReviews states heterozygous females with favourably skewed XCI may be unaffected or mildly affected — i.e. humans who simply make more wild-type CDKL5 per brain are healthier. No human carrier of a poison-exon-ablating CDKL5 variant found.  
Domains: n/a — product is the unaltered 960-aa hCDKL5_1 protein (kinase domain 13-297, catalytic Ser135, ATP site 19-27/42 all intact); only transcript routing changes.  
Rescue: No CDKL5 poison-exon ASO found. Indirect: postnatal restoration of endogenous Cdkl5 to near-WT forebrain levels reversed hyperactivity, anxiety, social, motor and contextual-memory deficits and NMDAR signalling in male mice (working memory not reversed); in heterozygous females early reactivation reversed most phenotypes. Cross-target chemistry/architecture precedent only from SCN1A poison-exon ASOs.

**Suppress constitutive intron retention in CDKL5 pre-mRNA (anchor: retained_intron transcript ENST00000623610)** (uncertain) — Product quality is ideal but the recoverable reservoir is unquantified — an annotated retained_intron transcript of 2.1 kb is weak evidence that a therapeutically useful fraction of neuronal CDKL5 pre-mRNA sits in a rescuable intron-retaining pool.  
Natural: None found for CDKL5 intron-retention modifiers. Same surrogate as above: favourably skewed XCI females, and mosaic males with postzygotic variants, who have mild manifestations — both humans whose benefit comes purely from more wild-type CDKL5.  
Domains: n/a — unaltered 960-aa protein; no coding change, kinase domain 13-297 and the disordered C-terminal regulatory regions (300-349, 382-566, 646-834, 848-960) preserved.  
Rescue: No CDKL5 intron-retention rescue found. Only the generic restoration evidence (JCI 143655; Cell Reports Medicine reversal study) showing that raising wild-type CDKL5 postnatally is beneficial.

**Redirect isoform choice toward full-length hCDKL5_1 (alternative last exon / 3'-end switch away from hCDKL5_5, or away from exon-17-containing isoforms)** (uncertain) — The target is locatable, but the arithmetic is unfavourable: hCDKL5_5 is testis-restricted in adults and exon-17 isoforms are only ~10% of brain transcripts, so even complete redirection buys a small fraction more full-length protein — well below the ~50%-of-neurons restoration that moved phenotypes in vivo.  
Natural: A boy with early-onset seizures carried a variant in an alternative CDKL5 transcript, arguing the non-canonical isoforms are not inert. Conversely, truncating variants in the 170 nt of the brain transcript's last exon that is non-coding in other transcripts gave a range from relatively mild (pharmaco-responsive epilepsy, independent walking) to severe — direct human evidence that C-terminal/3'-end identity matters clinically.  
Domains: n/a for the intended product (unaltered 960-aa hCDKL5_1). The displaced isoforms differ at residues ~905-960 (UniProt isoform 1, 1030 aa vs isoform 2, 960 aa) and exon 17 adds 41 aa in the disordered C-terminal region — i.e. the switch trades C-terminal regulatory tails, not catalytic domain.  
Rescue: None found for a CDKL5 isoform-switch ASO (searches: CDKL5 alternative last exon ASO; hCDKL5_5 exon 21 splice switching).

**Relieve 5'UTR translational repression (structured element / IRES-dependent initiation; CDKL5 TSS cluster 202up/202/205/205down)** (plausible) — A human 5'UTR variant with a ~25% translational effect and a documented IRES/G-quadruplex-rich element make this the best-evidenced non-splicing handle, but the absence of a predicted uORF means the ASO would have to de-repress structure (G-quadruplex pG41, GC>60%, dG -91 to -101 kcal/mol) — a harder, less precedented ASO action, and the fold-change ceiling may be small.  
Natural: A CDD patient carries a C>T at position -189 in the CDKL5 5'UTR that reduced reporter activity ~25%, i.e. a human in whom a modest 5'UTR-driven drop in CDKL5 translation tracks with disease. This is the human proof that this element is rate-limiting; no human with a gain-of-translation 5'UTR variant was found.  
Domains: n/a — mRNA sequence and abundance unchanged; the protein made is unaltered full-length CDKL5.  
Rescue: No ASO rescue found. Supporting biology: eIF4B silencing cut CDKL5 protein ~50% in SH-SY5Y cells, showing translation-factor-dependent control of CDKL5 output; no significant uORF start sites were predicted, so a classical uORF-blocking ASO has no documented target.

**3'UTR / alternative-polyadenylation stabilisation (6.6 kb hCDKL5_1 3'UTR, miRNA-site masking)** (uncertain) — The product spec is satisfied and the 6.6 kb 3'UTR is a large target surface, but no single element is shown to be rate-limiting, and masking the gene's own brake is the mechanism least compatible with the bounded upregulation window implied by CDKL5 dosage-sensitivity concerns.  
Natural: None found — no human carrier of a CDKL5 3'UTR variant with a defined expression or clinical effect was retrievable (searches: CDKL5 3'UTR variant phenotype; CDKL5 miRNA regulation neurons).  
Domains: n/a — coding sequence untouched; only 3'UTR length/occupancy changes, protein is unaltered 960-aa CDKL5.  
Rescue: None found. Only the observation that the brain isoform's >6.6 kb 3'UTR contains multiple putative miRNA sites and 'may suggest additional, previously unrecognised modes of regulation of CDKL5'.

**Transcriptional upregulation via a natural antisense transcript (antagoNAT) at the CDKL5 locus** (uncertain) — No cis-antisense or repressive regulatory RNA at the human CDKL5 locus was retrievable, so there is currently no ASO target here even though promoter-level de-repression is clearly capable of delivering the desired product.  
Natural: none found  
Domains: n/a — would increase transcription of the normal gene; unaltered protein.  
Rescue: None found for a NAT-directed ASO. The closest validated locus-level de-repression is epigenetic, not antisense: dCas9-TET1CD at the CDKL5 promoter gave 20.7-fold reactivation of the silenced allele (14.5% of total) in SH-SY5Y, and with VP64 reached >60% expression from the inactive allele with only one apparent off-target gene (CNTNAP2).

**Reactivate the silenced wild-type allele by targeting XIST / X-inactivation machinery (listed 'poor')** (plausible) — The biological premise is now validated in a heterozygous female model and in patient cells, but the validated agent is a locus-specific epigenome editor, not an XIST ASO — a genome-scale X de-repression has a far worse specificity profile than the gene-targeted version that already works.  
Natural: Favourably skewed X-inactivation females who are unaffected or mildly affected are the human proof that expressing the wild-type allele in more neurons is therapeutic. There is no human equivalent of global XIST loss in postnatal neurons, so the safety side has no human anchor.  
Domains: n/a — unaltered full-length CDKL5 where the wild-type allele is reactivated; in the complementary cell population the reactivated allele is the mutant one, whose product depends on the patient's variant.  
Rescue: Locus-targeted (not XIST-wide) reactivation works: a dual-effector CRISPR epigenome editor delivered by ICV restored Cdkl5 protein, re-engaged downstream signalling and rescued motor and cognitive deficits in heterozygous Cdkl5 E6del mice, and normalised network activity in patient-derived neural stem cells and cortical organoids (preprint, 2025).

**Stabilise the mutant transcript by blocking NMD/EJC deposition (poor; product is a TRUNCATED protein)** (likely poor) — Only the latest C-terminal truncations have any human evidence of mildness, and even those span mild to severe, so stabilising a truncated kinase is variant-specific, unpredictable, and in the kinase-domain-deleting majority has no plausible catalytic product.  
Natural: Humans with truncating CDKL5 variants are the equivalent, and position dictates phenotype: truncations in the terminal region may be less severe, p.Arg970Ter in the last exon was reported with late-onset seizures and occurs in a heterozygous female in ExAC, and truncations in the brain-transcript-specific 3' region range from relatively mild to severe. Truncations upstream, i.e. anywhere removing the kinase domain, give full CDD severity.  
Domains: Depends on PTC position. A PTC before residue ~297 destroys the protein kinase domain (13-297), the ATP-binding site (19-27, 42) and catalytic Ser135 — no catalytic product. A PTC after ~297 keeps the intact kinase domain but deletes disordered regulatory regions (300-349, 382-566, 646-834, 848-960) that carry regulatory phosphosites Ser407/479/720/761 and the isoform-defining 905-960 tail; only the latest truncations (losing ~905-960 / the 1030-aa isoform tail) have a mild human precedent.  
Rescue: None found for NMD-blocking ASOs in CDKL5 (searches: CDKL5 NMD inhibition ASO; CDKL5 PTC readthrough rescue).

**Exon skipping / multi-exon skipping (not applicable; product would be an internally deleted protein)** (likely poor) — There is no variant, no shared mutant transcript and no target exon in this lesion, and any internally deleted kinase fails the stated product requirement of the unaltered MANE coding sequence while doing nothing for mosaic silencing.  
Natural: No human with an in-frame internal CDKL5 deletion that preserves function was found. The only in-frame-ish natural variation is the cryptic exon-11 splice site removing 51 nt / 17 aa (<5% of brain transcripts) and the 41-aa exon-17 insertion — neither is a disease-rescuing deletion. Human data instead show that kinase-domain-disrupting variants, including missense in 13-297, cause full CDD.  
Domains: Any skip within exons encoding residues 13-297 removes part of the catalytic kinase fold, the ATP pocket (19-27, 42) or Ser135 — catalytically dead. Skips in the C-terminal half delete disordered regulatory segments and regulatory phosphosites (Ser407/479/720/761), which in humans ranges from mild (post-~905) to severe.  
Rescue: none found

**Modulate a downstream effector instead of CDKL5 (poor; no change to CDKL5 RNA or protein)** (likely poor) — It abandons the stated goal of increasing full-length CDKL5 and is symptomatic, but it has the only in-vivo pharmacological rescue among the 'poor' classes and is the rational fallback if no CDKL5 cis handle survives screening.  
Natural: n/a — no CDKL5 product is made. Human-relevant pathway anchor: ganaxolone is in CDD trials including infants 6 months to <2 years, i.e. symptomatic modulation is clinically tractable but not restorative.  
Domains: n/a — CDKL5 protein unchanged; an altered transcript of a second gene is the product.  
Rescue: KCC2 activation during postnatal development alleviated long-term deficits in CDKL5-deficient mice, giving a concrete downstream node with in vivo rescue; also cell-type-specific compensation of CDKL5 activity has been mapped in mouse brain.

Ranking: Best products are those yielding unaltered 960-aa CDKL5: (1) poison-exon/non-productive-splicing suppression — ideal product, real NMD-biotype anchor, needs discovery; (2) 5'UTR de-repression — only human variant evidence that the element is rate-limiting; (3) XCI/allele reactivation — now rescued in heterozygous female mice and patient organoids, but gene-targeted editors beat XIST ASOs; then intron retention, NAT, 3'UTR. Isoform switching is too low-flux. Truncated-protein mechanisms rank last.

## ASO design evidence
**Suppress a non-productive / NMD-coupled CDKL5 splicing event (poison exon or AS-NMD exon)**  
Published: none found — no ASO of any kind published against CDKL5. The only CDKL5 splicing therapeutic retrieved is a modified U1 snRNA (not an ASO) correcting individual splice-site variants, including c.2376+5G>A which causes exon 16 skipping and NMD with ~80% protein loss in patient lymphocytes (PMC6747535). Searches: 'CDKL5 antisense oligonucleotide' (PubMed, HTTP 429 then re-run via web), web 'CDKL5 splice-switching oligonucleotide OR morpholino exon skipping rescue' — no CDKL5 hits.  
Target evidence: No validated CDKL5 poison exon found. Hector 2016 characterised the gene exhaustively and reported no NMD-coupled isoform; the only alternative internal event, exon 17, adds 123 nt (41 aa, in-frame) at ~10% of brain transcripts, so it is not a poison exon. A 2026 genome-wide screen of ~3,000 developmentally regulated AS-NMD exons nominating neurodevelopmental splice-switching targets (GRIA2, FOXP1, SNRPB, DLG4, SYNGAP1; 308 Gene4Epilepsy genes) does not mention CDKL5. Ensembl does annotate one NMD-biotype transcript, CDKL5-214/ENST00000977893 (14 exons), and one retained-intron transcript, CDKL5-206/ENST00000623610, but neither is quantified anywhere I could retrieve. Searches tried: PubMed 'CDKL5 poison exon antisense oligonucleotide upregulation', 'CDKL5 isoforms splice variants', 'CDKL5 alternative splicing isoforms exon 16 exon 21 brain'; Europe PMC 'CDKL5 poison exon antisense oligonucleotide' (503 error, could not complete); web 'CDKL5 poison exon OR nonsense-mediated decay non-productive splicing isoform', 'poison exon human brain atlas catalogue'; direct text query of the JCI AS-NMD paper and the Pigini poison-exon preprint (preprint PDF returned HTTP 429 twice, not read). Conclusion: this is a discovery project, not a design project — flux through any non-productive CDKL5 event is unquantified and may be near zero.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: none found specific to CDKL5. General precedent: divalent/branched oligonucleotide scaffolds in which two fully modified phosphorothioate arms are joined by a neutral linker give months-long, brain-wide activity after one CSF dose in mice and NHPs (divalent siRNA, HTT); US 11,896,669 'Branched oligonucleotides'. This is delivery/architecture precedent only, from siRNA not splice-switching ASO.  
Amenability: Unknown and probably poor. Target evidence is missing, so there is no measurable non-productive flux to redirect. Note the genuine amenability features that do exist: five alternative first exons (1, 1a, 1b, 1c, 1d) over 18 kb all using the same ATG in exon 2, with 1/1a/1b brain-specific — so any 5'-region ASO must tolerate three leaders; and hCDKL5_1 is already ~90% of brain transcript, leaving only ~10% headroom from isoform redistribution even if a handle were found.

**Suppress constitutive intron retention in CDKL5 pre-mRNA**  
Published: none found for CDKL5. No intron-retention-directed ASO for this gene exists. Searches as above plus 'CDKL5 antisense oligonucleotide'.  
Target evidence: Not demonstrated for CDKL5. Ensembl lists a single retained_intron transcript (CDKL5-206/ENST00000623610, 1 exon), which is an annotation fragment with no expression quantification I could retrieve. Hector 2016's experimental transcript profiling of human and mouse CDKL5 describes no constitutive retained intron. Background literature establishes that intron retention is widespread (detectable in up to three-quarters of multiexonic genes) and increases during neuronal differentiation, with 88.7% of differentially retained introns rising from ESC to mature neuron — so a CDKL5 retained intron is plausible a priori but has never been measured. Searches tried: web 'CDKL5 intron retention neurons RNA-seq retained intron transcript' (returned only generic IR literature, no CDKL5); PubMed 'CDKL5 isoforms splice variants'; Ensembl transcript table. No percent-intron-retention value for any CDKL5 intron in any human neuronal dataset was found.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: none found for CDKL5 intron retention; divalent branched scaffold precedent as above (US 11,896,669; divalent siRNA CNS data) is architecture-only.  
Amenability: Cannot be assessed without a candidate intron. Practical note: the readout differs from poison-exon work — it requires intron-level percent-retention from nuclear/cytoplasmic fractionated human neuronal RNA-seq, and a retained-intron pool only helps if it is nuclear-detained rather than already degraded. Confounder specific to CDKL5: the brain isoform terminates in what older papers call intron 18, so naive intron-retention callers will score the normal terminal-exon read-through of the dominant isoform as 'retention'.

**Redirect isoform choice toward full-length hCDKL5_1 (alternative last exon / 3'-end switch)**  
Published: none found. No ASO against CDKL5 exon 17, exon 19, intron 18 or the exon19→20 junction. Searches: PubMed 'CDKL5 isoforms splice variants', 'Hector CDKL5 transcript isoforms nomenclature'; web 'CDKL5 splice-switching oligonucleotide OR morpholino'.  
Target evidence: Demonstrated and quantified — but the direction assumed in the brief is wrong. The brain-predominant isoform hCDKL5_1 is the 18-exon transcript ending in exon 19 with a 6.6 kb 3'UTR (~90% of brain transcripts); the 115 kDa isoform that splices exon 19 onward to exons 20/21/22 with an 86-nt 3'UTR is hCDKL5_5, which is adult-testis-specific (also fetal brain). Williamson 2012 independently found this alternative-C-terminus 107 kDa isoform to be the major CDKL5 transcript in human brain and all tissues except testis. Clinically, missense, nonsense and splice variants in the final three exons are benign, so hCDKL5_5 'is likely to have little or no neurologic significance'. Therefore forcing splicing through to the long 21/22-exon form would convert the functional neuronal isoform into a neurologically irrelevant one. The only legitimate version of this mechanism in brain is suppressing exon 17 inclusion to convert hCDKL5_2 to hCDKL5_1 — maximum headroom ~10%, and exon 17 is in-frame so hCDKL5_2 is already productive, meaning total productive mRNA would not rise at all.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: none found. A dual-arm oligo simultaneously masking the exon-17 acceptor and an exon-19 element is conceivable but has no precedent; only divalent branched siRNA CNS scaffolds (US 11,896,669) exist as architecture precedent.  
Amenability: Mechanically the most locatable target in the gene — the alternative 3' end is experimentally mapped with two canonical AATAAA signals in exon 19 at 6.6 kb and 9.7 kb past the stop codon, giving 9.7 kb and 12.8 kb transcripts, the 12.8 kb XL form being less abundant. But the therapeutic headroom is the problem, not the target: the desired isoform is already dominant. Deprioritise.

**Relieve 5'UTR / uORF or IRES-associated translational repression**  
Published: none found against the CDKL5 5'UTR or UTex2. No ASO publication for CDKL5 of any mechanism. Searches as listed above.  
Target evidence: Partly demonstrated, but not as a uORF. A dedicated study of CDKL5 translational regulation found that two independent algorithms gave no significant prediction of upstream start sites in the CDKL5 transcript leaders — i.e. uORFs are probably not a CDKL5 regulatory feature. What was found instead is a complex leader built from exon 1 plus a 162-nt untranslated distal region of exon 2 (UTex2), evidence for cap-independent initiation, and a proposed CDKL5 IRES, supported by a loss-of-function C>T at position -189 in the 5'UTR in a patient with CDD-consistent symptoms. A -189 LoF variant means the element is translation-promoting, not repressive: masking it would lower, not raise, CDKL5. No repressive 5'UTR element has been mapped. Searches tried: web 'CDKL5 5'UTR upstream open reading frame translational regulation exon 1 1a 1b'; PMID 37964795 fetched (PubMed page returned only a cookie notice, abstract read via search snippets only — flag as partially retrieved); Hector 2016 full text for leader structure.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: none found. A multi-oligo approach would in fact be forced here for a different reason: three brain leaders (exons 1, 1a, 1b) converge on the same ATG in exon 2, so a leader-specific oligo covers only part of the transcript pool, and only UTex2 is common to all.  
Amenability: Weak as written. The only common, fully shared, accessible 5' element across brain leaders is UTex2 (162 nt), and the available genetic evidence points to it being required rather than inhibitory. Also note the brief's desired product is defined as mRNA abundance, which a translational-de-repression ASO does not change — the readout would have to be protein per transcript, not junction ratios.

**3'UTR / alternative-polyadenylation stabilisation**  
Published: none found against the CDKL5 3'UTR. Closest mechanistic precedent in a related gene family is CDK5R1 (not CDKL5), where hnRNPA2/B1 and nELAV bind a U-rich 3'UTR element and oppositely regulate expression — do not confuse the two genes. Searches: 'CDKL5 microRNA', 'CDKL5 antisense oligonucleotide', 3'UTR query above.  
Target evidence: The element space is demonstrated and unusually large; no individual functional element is yet validated. The dominant brain isoform carries a >6.6 kb 3'UTR in exon 19, with two canonical AATAAA signals (at 6.6 kb and 9.7 kb past the stop codon) producing 9.7 kb and lower-abundance 12.8 kb transcripts — so a genuine competing-polyA choice exists and is measurable. Hector 2016 explicitly proposes the large 3'UTR as a previously unrecognised mode of CDKL5 regulation and a possible dendritic-trafficking determinant, and notes putative miRNA binding sites in the 3'UTR whose importance 'is not clearly established for CDKL5'. One specific miRNA interaction is experimentally supported outside neurons: miR-320b targets the CDKL5 3'UTR by dual-luciferase assay in breast cancer cells. Searches tried: PubMed 'CDKL5 microRNA'; web 'CDKL5 3'UTR long 6.6 kb regulatory element mRNA stability neurons polyadenylation'. No neuronal 3'UTR reporter dissection or half-life measurement for CDKL5 was found.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: none found for CDKL5. Relevant general point: a 6.6 kb UTR is large enough that a single steric oligo is unlikely to control stability, so a multi-site cocktail or a divalent construct with two arms against two separate destabilising elements is the realistic architecture — precedent only from divalent branched oligonucleotide CNS scaffolds (US 11,896,669).  
Amenability: Best combination of 'element definitely exists' and 'target is accessible' of any mechanism here, and it leaves the coding sequence untouched. Two concrete handles: (i) steric block of the proximal AATAAA to force the distal site and the longer UTR, with direction of effect to be determined empirically; (ii) masking destabilising/miRNA elements identified by tiling. Main liability is the one the brief flags: CDKL5 is dosage-sensitive and masking the cell's own brake removes the self-limiting ceiling, so a bounded window must be engineered by dose, not by mechanism.

**Transcriptional upregulation via a natural antisense transcript (antagoNAT) at the CDKL5 locus**  
Published: none found for CDKL5. The only de-repression experiment at this locus used dCas9-TET1CD epigenetic editing with three gRNAs at the CDKL5 promoter, not oligonucleotides: no antisense oligonucleotides were used. Searches as above.  
Target evidence: Not demonstrated. No cis-antisense or repressive regulatory RNA at the human CDKL5 locus (Xp22.13) was retrievable. What is established is a large (>1 kb) CpG island spanning the CDKL5 transcription start site, 53% methylated at baseline in the core promoter of a female neuronal line, and five promoters driving the five alternative first exons over 18 kb — so the promoter region is epigenetically regulated, but by DNA methylation rather than by any identified NAT. Searches tried: web 'CDKL5 natural antisense transcript lncRNA promoter upstream locus Xp22' (returned only generic NAT literature plus the CDKL5 methylation-editing paper); PubMed 'CDKL5 microRNA' (found LINC00680 acting as a miR-320b sponge on CDKL5 in breast cancer — a trans-acting lncRNA in a cancer line, not a cis NAT and not neuronal). Conclusion: no antagoNAT target exists on current evidence.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: none found at the CDKL5 locus. Note the methylation-editing precedent is itself multi-target (three gRNAs plus, in the synergistic arm, a second effector), which argues any promoter-directed de-repression here will need combinatorial rather than single-agent action.  
Amenability: Promoter de-repression is achievable in principle at this locus — dCas9-TET1CD reached ~38% of the active allele, and adding VP64 exceeded 60%, with a 20.7-fold increase in silenced-allele expression by amplicon sequencing, in SH-SY5Y — but that was accomplished by demethylation, a modality ASOs do not provide. Without an identified RNA intermediate there is nothing for an oligonucleotide to bind. Treat as target-discovery (CAGE/nuclear RNA-seq strand-specific sequencing at Xp22.13 in human neurons) before any design.

**Reactivate the silenced wild-type allele via XIST / X-inactivation machinery**  
Published: Yes — an Xist-directed ASO is published and is the only ASO relevant to any mechanism on this list. Sequence and name are not given in the retrieved abstract; the compound is described only as 'an Xist antisense oligonucleotide' used with 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine (PMID 29282321). Related IP: US 10,718,022 and US 11,268,150 'Compositions and methods for Xi chromosome reactivation'. In vivo work to date used genetic Xist ablation rather than the ASO, so no CNS ASO dose-response for Xist was retrieved.  
Target evidence: Demonstrated, with the clearest quantification of any mechanism here, and CDKL5 is explicitly named as an intended indication. An Xist ASO combined with a DNA-methylation inhibitor achieved 30,000-fold MECP2 upregulation from the inactive X in cultured cells; brain-specific genetic Xist ablation plus short-term 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine modelled the synergy in vivo without evident toxicity, and the Xi was selectively reactivated. Independently, CDKL5 is confirmed subject to XCI with monoallelic expression and does not naturally escape, so there is a silenced wild-type allele to recover in roughly half of neurons. Postnatal maintenance dependence is supported by evidence that transcriptional repression on the inactive X is perturbed in mouse brain after Xist deletion.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: Strong multi-target precedent, and it is intrinsic rather than optional: the published approach is explicitly a 'mixed modality' combination of an oligonucleotide with a small molecule, because neither alone reactivates efficiently. No single linked oligonucleotide construct for Xi reactivation was found; divalent branched scaffolds (US 11,896,669) remain the only linked-architecture precedent and are untested on XIST.  
Amenability: XIST is an abundant, nuclear, chromatin-associated lncRNA — a favourable substrate for a nuclear-acting gapmer, and this is the only mechanism whose target is known rather than hypothetical. The disqualifying property is selectivity, not druggability: reactivation is not allele-directed, so in the ~half of neurons whose active X carries the mutant allele it de-represses the mutant copy, and it releases hundreds of unrelated X-linked genes. The companion observation that CDKL5 varies ~2.5-fold between healthy individuals and has a median detection rate of ~69% across adult brain regions suggests the required reactivation may be modest, which argues for the lowest effective exposure rather than maximal reactivation.

**Exon skipping / multi-exon skipping of CDKL5 coding exons**  
Published: none found. No exon-skipping ASO or PMO has been published for CDKL5. Searches: web 'CDKL5 splice-switching oligonucleotide OR morpholino OR antisense oligonucleotide exon skipping rescue splicing mutation'; PubMed 'CDKL5 antisense oligonucleotide', 'CDKL5 isoforms splice variants'.  
Target evidence: n/a — no variant and no target exon were supplied, and the enumeration returned no exon-based design space, so there is nothing to skip. Included only to record the negative. Note the one circumstance where CDKL5 exon-level work is real: endogenous skipping of exon 16 is pathogenic (caused by c.2376+5G>A, leading to NMD and ~80% protein loss), i.e. skipping in this gene destroys rather than restores function.  
Coordinated: Not applicable, and not assessable: with no block of adjacent target exons defined there is no adjacent pair to test. No CDKL5 coordinated- or double-skipping report exists; searches run were 'CDKL5 splice-switching oligonucleotide OR morpholino OR antisense oligonucleotide exon skipping', 'CDKL5 isoforms splice variants', 'CDKL5 alternative splicing isoforms exon 16 exon 21 brain' (0 hits), and the web poison-exon/splicing queries above. The only naturally occurring multi-element skip documented in CDKL5 is the cryptic splice site within exon 11 used by hCDKL5_3/_4 that removes 51 bases (17 aa) in <5% of brain transcripts — a partial-exon event, not oligonucleotide-induced.  
Linked: none found for CDKL5. Multi-oligo cocktail precedent exists in DMD but is irrelevant here because no exon is being removed.  
Amenability: Not applicable. CDKL5 encodes a kinase whose catalytic domain is conserved across all isoforms and whose pathogenic missense variants cluster in that catalytic domain, so internal coding deletions are expected to destroy function; and exon skipping would do nothing about mosaic X-inactivation, which is what drives the deficit. Actively design away from inducing skipping of CDKL5 coding exons as an off-mechanism hazard when screening any intronic oligo.

Non-transferable benchmarks: Three results will be misread as expectations for CDKL5. (1) Zorevunersen/STK-001 for Dravet: a poison-exon ASO that raises productive SCN1A and Nav1.1, now with NEJM-published clinical data (March 2026, PMID 41780062) across 81 patients at 10-70 mg and FDA Breakthrough designation. Its efficacy rests on a validated, highly-used SCN1A non-productive exon; CDKL5 has no demonstrated poison exon at all (absent from a genome-wide AS-NMD screen that nominated GRIA2, FOXP1, SNRPB, DLG4, SYNGAP1), so zorevunersen gives CDKL5 only chemistry, intrathecal dosing, safety and regulatory precedent — zero expectation of fold-upregulation. (2) The GRIA2 exon-14N ASO result (up to 3.8-fold increase in non-NMD isoform) is a per-target number set by that exon's inclusion rate; it is not a CDKL5 ceiling. (3) The 30,000-fold MECP2 reactivation from Xist ASO plus Aza is a cultured-cell, allele-of-origin measurement starting from near-zero baseline on the inactive X, and it required a co-administered small molecule; it is not a CNS fold-change and cannot be quoted as expected CDKL5 protein gain. Also: divalent/branched oligonucleotide CNS durability data come from siRNA knockdown of HTT, and knockdown potency does not predict splice-switching or steric-block upregulation potency. Finally, the dCas9-TET1CD figures at the CDKL5 promoter (~38%, >60% with VP64, 20.7-fold) are the only CDKL5-specific upregulation numbers retrieved, but they were produced by epigenetic editing in SH-SY5Y, not by an oligonucleotide, and must not be presented as an ASO-achievable target. Dose ceiling to respect regardless of mechanism: duplications spanning CDKL5 are associated with neurobehavioural alterations, so a bounded upregulation window is required, with the ~2.5-fold inter-individual range in healthy human brain as the practical reference band.

Proposed designs:
- Discovery-first (prerequisite): fractionated nuclear/cytoplasmic RNA-seq ± NMD inhibition in human CDD and control iPSC neurons to quantify any non-productive CDKL5 event before committing.
- Lead: 2'-MOE PS 18-mer steric-block tiling walk across the exon-19 proximal AATAAA (6.6 kb) and 1 kb flanks to force distal polyadenylation; ~60 oligos, 10-nt step.
- Parallel arm: tiling screen of conserved destabilising/miRNA elements across the 6.6 kb exon-19 3'UTR (incl. miR-320b site); ~150 oligos, hCDKL5_1-specific.
- Backstop only: nuclear-acting XIST gapmer dose-titrated with a DNMT inhibitor, read out allele-specifically for CDKL5; accept non-allele-selective reactivation as the known liability.
- Design-away control: confirm no candidate oligo induces skipping of CDKL5 coding exons (especially exon 16) or loss of UTex2.

## Delivery platforms
**Unconjugated 2'-MOE phosphorothioate ASO, intrathecal lumbar bolus (Ionis/Biogen-class chemistry)** — Naked single-strand ASO, 2'-O-methoxyethyl/PS backbone. Two payload modes both relevant here: RNase H1 gapmer (knockdown) and fully 2'-MOE steric-block splice switcher (the mode CDKL5 upregulation mechanisms require). No ligand; uptake is bulk CSF convection plus non-specific endocytosis.  
Reach: Yes for most required cells, unevenly. Cortex, cerebellar cortex, hippocampus, pons, medulla, thalamus, amygdala and spinal cord reach concentrations similar to spinal cord; deep nuclei (caudate/putamen) are >10-fold lower than cortex. Critically for CDD, single-cell profiling in NHP thalamus/caudate/putamen showed all neuronal subtypes knocked down as deep as or deeper than bulk tissue, with microglia deepest and endothelium weakest — so the deep-brain gap is an exposure gradient, not a neuron-exclusion problem. Retina and visual-pathway photoreceptors are NOT covered by intrathecal dosing and need a separate intravitreal arm. Heart/periphery get negligible exposure after IT (safety advantage, no cardiac liability).  
Human PD: Zilganersen (ION-373, GFAP): 25 or 50 mg IT q12w, 53 patients aged 2–53; plasma GFAP −33.6% (p=0.003), 10-m walk gait speed LSM difference +33.3% (p=0.041) at week 61; FDA-approved 3 Sep 2026 (Zanvastro). Diranersen/BIIB080 (MAPT): 60 or 115 mg IT; CSF total tau reduced ~50–60% from baseline across doses over 76 weeks. Tofersen: CSF SOD1 −37% at 100 mg vs no change on placebo (−1%/−27%/−21% at 20/40/60 mg) — a dose-response worth noting because it shows naked IT ASO target engagement can be modest even in the best-exposed tissue (spinal motor neurons). Zorevunersen (TANGO splice-switch upregulation, Dravet children): clinical and safety data exist but no public human molecular PD (no human SCN1A/Nav1.1 measurement) — so there is NO human PD number anywhere for an upregulation ASO.  
Dose: Pediatric precedent is strong: nusinersen 12 mg IT ×4 loading then q4 months from newborn; zilganersen 25–50 mg q12 weeks, label extended below age 2 by PK modelling; zorevunersen 70 mg (day 1, wk 8) then 45 mg (wk 24, wk 40) in children 2–<18. Plan on q8–16-week lumbar punctures under sedation for a CDD infant/child population.  
Tox: Manageable and characterised in children: vomiting, back pain, headache, post-lumbar-puncture syndrome, and aseptic meningitis (zilganersen label warning). Communicating hydrocephalus requiring VP shunt reported post-marketing with nusinersen. Preclinical hazard to design against: PS-ASO macrovesicular vacuolation of hippocampal neurons in NHP — directly relevant since hippocampus is a required CDD tissue. Sequence-dependent neurotoxicity screening is mandatory.  
Access: Fully buildable in-house. Chemistry is generic/off-patent-adjacent, CROs synthesise GMP MOE-PS, and the n-of-1/individualised ASO regulatory path (FDA individualised-ASO guidance) plus academic IT dosing infrastructure exist. No licence needed for the delivery route. This is the default for a first CDD program.

**TfR1 antibody/Fab–oligonucleotide conjugates, intravenous (Denali OTV; Dyne FORCE; Avidity AOC)** — Anti-human-TfR1 antibody or Fab covalently linked to an oligonucleotide payload (Denali OTV: maleimide-C6 linker at engineered Ser239 of a TfR antibody; Dyne FORCE: Fab-ASO; Avidity AOC: mAb-siRNA or mAb-PMO). Receptor-mediated transcytosis across brain endothelium, then receptor-mediated uptake by parenchymal cells.  
Reach: Preclinically yes, and more uniformly than intrathecal naked ASO. Systemic OTV in human-TfR1 knock-in mice gave sustained Malat1 knockdown across multiple CNS regions and in endothelial cells, neurons, astrocytes, microglia and oligodendrocytes, with more uniform biodistribution than naked ASO; robust activity also in cynomolgus macaques. Dyne reported FORCE Fab-ASO CNS delivery in mice and NHP with broader brain distribution than intrathecal unconjugated ASO. Caveat for CDD: OTV also knocked down target in quadriceps and cardiac muscle — i.e. heart is exposed, which is a new safety surface an IT ASO does not have, and TfR1 is on erythroid precursors (reticulocyte/anaemia risk requires engineered monovalent, affinity-tuned binding).  
Human PD: No human CNS PD yet. DNL628 (OTV:MAPT) dosed its first Phase 1b patient in March 2026; initial CSF tau/p-tau biomarker data guided to 1H 2027. Human PD for TfR1 conjugates exists only in muscle disease (Avidity/Dyne DM1, DMD, FSHD programs). Treat brain exposure in humans as unproven as of today.  
Dose: IV infusion, anticipated monthly to quarterly based on OTV preclinical durability and the Phase 1b design (adverse events assessed over 37 weeks). Avoids repeated lumbar punctures — a real advantage in a severely disabled paediatric population.  
Tox: Known class risks: TfR1-mediated reticulocyte depletion/anaemia, infusion reactions, immunogenicity against the antibody, and on-target activity in heart and skeletal muscle. Paediatric exposure data: none. Repeat IV dosing in infants with a BBB-crossing antibody is unprecedented.  
Access: Not buildable in-house in any realistic timeframe. OTV is Denali-proprietary and clinically unpartnered for rare paediatric CNS upregulation; FORCE is Dyne-proprietary; AOC is Avidity-proprietary. Route is partnering/licensing only, and all three are prioritising large indications. Realistic posture: keep as a second-generation delivery upgrade and open business-development conversations now, but do not make the CDD program contingent on it.

**Peptide–oligonucleotide conjugates for systemic CNS delivery (LDLR/ApoB11, ApoE-derived BPP)** — Short receptor-binding or BBB-penetrating peptide conjugated to an ASO or PMO. ApoB11 binds LDL receptor; ApoE-derived BPP peptides exploit lipoprotein-receptor transcytosis. Payload can be steric-block PMO, which is mechanism-compatible with CDKL5 upregulation.  
Reach: Preclinical rodent brain and spinal cord only. ApoB11-ASO given systemically crossed the BBB with sustained CNS retention; BPP-PMO reached brain and spinal cord of SMN2 transgenic mice. No cell-type-resolved neuronal coverage in primates. Peripheral tissue exposure (liver, kidney) is high; cardiac data not retrieved.  
Human PD: None found. Searched for peptide-conjugated oligonucleotide CNS clinical data; the clinically advanced peptide conjugates (PepGen-class EDO/CPP-PMO) are muscle programs and I retrieved no human CNS PD for any peptide-oligo conjugate.  
Dose: Undefined in humans. Rodent work uses repeated systemic (IV/SC) dosing; translational dose and interval unknown.  
Tox: Historical class liability is cationic/arginine-rich CPP nephrotoxicity and complement/infusion effects; the lipoprotein-receptor peptides above were reported tolerable in mice but there is no primate or human safety package.  
Access: Most open class commercially — several of these peptides are academic publications with licensable or freely implementable chemistry, so an internal build is feasible. But it is a discovery-stage delivery bet: a CDD program choosing this accepts 3–5 years of delivery derisking on top of payload discovery.

**Divalent/branched and lipid-conjugated siRNA, intrathecal (Alnylam mivelsiran; Atalanta di-siRNA) and subcutaneous RNAi (Arrowhead TRiM)** — Chemically stabilised siRNA, either lipophilic-conjugated (mivelsiran, IT) or two siRNAs covalently branched (di-siRNA, IT), or Arrowhead's TRiM constructs including a subcutaneous CNS-directed design. Payload is RNAi — Argonaute-dependent, cytoplasmic, degradative.  
Reach: Yes, and this class has the best-documented deep-brain reach of any clinical CNS oligo platform; di-siRNA is explicitly positioned for historically inaccessible deep structures and showed ~6 months durability preclinically. Arrowhead's subcutaneous ARO-MAPT achieved 70–80% MAPT mRNA reduction across 12 NHP brain regions (up to 85% in cortex) after three weekly SC doses, which if replicated in humans would be the first non-invasive route to broad neuronal coverage.  
Human PD: Strong, and the best human numbers in CNS oligonucleotides: mivelsiran (ALN-APP, IT) single dose lowered CSF sAPPβ by up to 90% within 2 weeks with >70% maintained ≥3 months, and 50/75 mg single doses remained ~30% below baseline at 1 year; multiple-dose peak CSF sAPPβ −84.5% at month 1, −61.1% sustained at month 10, with no CSF protein or WBC elevation. Atalanta ATL-101 is IND-stage with no human data. ARO-ATXN2 is in Phase 1/2a; ARO-MAPT human readout guided to 2H 2026.  
Dose: Mivelsiran: intrathecal, dosing intervals of 6 months or longer are plausible given 1-year PD persistence. ARO-MAPT: subcutaneous, weekly loading then maintenance. Far fewer procedures than a q8–12-week ASO.  
Tox: Mivelsiran single-dose Phase 1: mild-to-moderate AEs, no CSF protein or pleocytosis signal. Class risks remain lumbar-puncture-related and sequence-dependent off-target; paediatric CNS siRNA experience is essentially nil.  
Access: Platform-proprietary (Alnylam, Atalanta, Arrowhead) — licensing only, and for CDD the licence would buy a modality that cannot execute the required mechanism. Correct use of this evidence: it sets the bar for how much CNS exposure and durability are achievable, and it supports a di-siRNA-style architecture only if CDD strategy shifts to NAT degradation or to a downstream knockdown target.

**Intravitreal unconjugated ASO (retina and visual-pathway arm)** — Naked steric-block or RNase H ASO injected into the vitreous; retinal cells take it up without a ligand. Required as a separate arm because the CDD tissue list includes retina and visual-pathway neurons, which intrathecal dosing does not reach.  
Reach: Yes — photoreceptors and inner retina. Sepofarsen reached foveal cone photoreceptors in CEP290-LCA10 patients. For the upregulation mechanism specifically, STK-002 (an OPA1 non-productive-exon ASO, the closest payload analogue to a CDKL5 TANGO ASO) increased OPA1 protein in retinal tissue of cynomolgus monkeys and rabbits at 4 weeks, persisting to 8 weeks in monkeys.  
Human PD: Sepofarsen Phase 1b/2: visual function and retinal structure peaked near 3 months post-injection with efficacy sustained at 15 months; durable single-injection improvement replicated in individual patients. STK-002 first patient dosed in Phase 1 in February 2026 — no human PD yet.  
Dose: Single or infrequent intravitreal injection; sepofarsen's durability (months to >1 year per injection) suggests 6–12-month intervals.  
Tox: Dose-dependent ocular AEs with sepofarsen (cataract, inflammation) but a manageable profile. Intravitreal injection in a child with CDD requires general anaesthesia, which must be weighed against the clinical weight of the visual phenotype.  
Access: Fully buildable in-house; same chemistry and manufacturing as the IT arm, only a different administration route. Low incremental cost once a lead CDKL5 upregulation ASO exists.

**Xist-directed X-reactivation (ASO plus DNMT inhibitor combination)** — Nuclear-acting ASO against XIST or Xist-interactome factors, combined with a small-molecule DNA methyltransferase inhibitor. Delivery vehicle would be a standard IT ASO plus a systemic small molecule — i.e. no new delivery technology is needed, which is why it is worth listing here.  
Reach: Delivery-wise yes: the ASO component uses the validated IT route with neuronal coverage described above. The limitation is biological, not delivery — reactivation must occur in the specific neurons that silenced the wild-type allele, and the DNMT inhibitor must reach the same neurons at a tolerated dose.  
Human PD: None. No clinical X-reactivation program retrieved.  
Dose: Undefined. Would pair repeat IT ASO (q8–16 weeks by analogy) with intermittent systemic DNMT inhibition — a chronic genotoxic/epigenetic exposure in children.  
Tox: The binding constraint. Genome-scale: de-repression of hundreds of X-linked genes, reactivation of the mutant allele in the other half of neurons, and paediatric chronic nucleoside DNMT-inhibitor exposure. Safety surface is far wider than any gene-specific ASO.  
Access: Buildable technically (ASO plus a marketed DNMT inhibitor) but not advisable as the lead. Keep as a mechanism-of-interest with an academic collaboration, not a development path.

**Intrathecal AAV9 gene replacement (non-oligo comparator that sets the efficacy bar)** — AAV9 carrying a functional human CDKL5 cassette, delivered into CSF. Included because it is the only CDKL5-specific CNS delivery program in active development and it defines what the oligo program must beat or complement.  
Reach: Intrathecal AAV9 targets CNS broadly; CDD-specific preclinical work exists, including secretable cell-penetrating TATk-CDKL5 designs intended to cross-correct neighbouring neurons and so address the mosaic coverage problem that any mutation-agnostic approach faces.  
Human PD: None. UX055 is in preclinical development per Ultragenyx; no clinical CDKL5 gene therapy data retrieved.  
Dose: One-time intrathecal administration.  
Tox: AAV class risks (immune response, dorsal root ganglion toxicity, hepatotoxicity) plus the CDKL5-specific overexpression liability — uncontrolled, permanent, non-titratable expression is exactly the wrong profile for a gene with a bounded upper safety limit.  
Access: Ultragenyx-proprietary. The strategic read for an ASO program: a titratable, reversible, dose-adjustable oligonucleotide that raises endogenous CDKL5 within a bounded window is differentiated against a one-shot non-titratable vector, and that argument depends on the ASO delivery platform being the conventional IT route whose dose can be adjusted.

Unconjugated baseline: Best unconjugated baseline is a fully 2'-MOE PS steric-block ASO given as an intrathecal lumbar bolus, with an intravitreal companion for the retinal arm. Dose and frequency, from approved/late-stage paediatric precedents: zilganersen 25 or 50 mg IT every 12 weeks (approved 3 Sep 2026, ages 2+ and extended below 2 by PK modelling); nusinersen 12 mg ×4 loading then every 4 months from newborn; zorevunersen — the closest payload analogue, a splice-switching upregulation ASO in children 2–<18 with a DEE — 70 mg at day 1 and week 8, then 45 mg at weeks 24 and 40. So plan 20–70 mg IT, q8–16 weeks, in children. Exposure numbers: CSF/target engagement of 37% (tofersen CSF SOD1, 100 mg) to ~50–60% (diranersen CSF tau) in well-perfused regions; cortex, cerebellum, hippocampus, brainstem and spinal cord are well covered, deep nuclei are >10-fold lower in drug concentration, but single-cell NHP data show all neuronal subtypes in thalamus/caudate/putamen are engaged as deeply as or more deeply than bulk tissue (PMID 40857674) — so required CDD neuron classes are reachable. Critical gap: there is no human molecular PD number for ANY upregulation ASO, so the CDKL5 fold-increase achievable in human neurons is unmeasured; the retina needs intravitreal (sepofarsen, STK-002 precedent), and no naked oligo reaches it from CSF.

Payload vs delivery: Delivery to CDD neurons is largely solved by plain intrathecal ASO; the unknown is payload. The 37–90% numbers quoted above all come from knockdown of highly amenable targets and say nothing about CDKL5 upregulation magnitude. No CDKL5 poison exon, NAT or uORF was retrievable — including in a 2026 brain poison-exon atlas with no CDKL5 entry. Target amenability, not delivery, dominates risk; conjugate platforms are also mechanism-incompatible (RNAi) or inaccessible.
