## Products
**P1 — no exon skipped; ASO-mediated EXCLUSION of the putative NMD-inducing exon ('exon 16x') in CDKL5 intron 16, from the patient's non-mutant allele** (plausible) — The product itself is perfect — unmodified canonical protein from the healthy allele — but the enabling non-productive splicing event is asserted only in a data-free patent, so existence and magnitude in human neurons are unverified.  
Natural: Product is the wild-type 960-aa brain isoform, so the human equivalent is any healthy CDKL5 allele. Dose-level equivalent: females with favourably skewed X-inactivation and post-zygotic mosaics, who are mild or unaffected — natural proof that raising canonical CDKL5 per neuron is sufficient. Upper bound exists: CDKL5 duplication carriers show autism, DD, macrocephaly without epilepsy (search snippet, full text not verified).  
Domains: None. No coding sequence altered: kinase domain 13-297, ATP site 19-27/42, catalytic D135 and the entire C-terminal regulatory/NLS-NES region are intact; output is the canonical brain isoform.  
Rescue: No CDKL5-specific ASO data found, published or in a pipeline. The only CDKL5 citation for this event is a Stoke patent naming 'exon 16x' in intron 16 with no supporting expression data. Indirect: adult re-expression of Cdkl5 at the endogenous locus in mice reverses most behavioural and NMDAR deficits (working memory not reversible). Searches tried: PubMed 'CDKL5 ASO poison exon', 'CDKL5 NMD exon', EuropePMC 'CDKL5 antisense oligonucleotide' (503), web 'CDKL5 TANGO 2025', 'Stoke CDKL5 pipeline'.

**P2 — no exon skipped; ASO redirection away from 3'-terminal non-productive CDKL5 isoforms (exon-20-containing transcripts whose frame stops near the end of exon 20) toward hCDKL5_1** (plausible) — Identical ideal product to P1 but the non-productive splicing event is at least documented in real human brain transcript data rather than only in a patent.  
Natural: Same as P1 — the product is the canonical 960-aa hCDKL5_1 brain protein. The non-productive isoform being suppressed is itself a normal constituent of human brain RNA, so its reduction has no truncated-protein equivalent to phenotype.  
Domains: None. Coding sequence unchanged; shifting isoform usage toward hCDKL5_1 (18 exons, 6.6 kb 3'UTR), the predominant human brain transcript.  
Rescue: None found for CDKL5. Event is described in human/mouse transcript characterisation: exon 20 inclusion terminates the frame near the end of exon 20 with splicing detected downstream. No ASO has been applied to it. Same search set as P1.

**P9 — no exon skipped; ASO targeting the 6.6 kb CDKL5 3'UTR / uORF to raise translation or stability of the existing canonical mRNA** (uncertain) — Perfect product, but no CDKL5 3'UTR regulatory element has been mapped to an ASO-actionable repressor, so there is currently no design handle.  
Natural: Product is canonical 960-aa protein; equivalent is the normal allele. Dosage equivalent again the mildly-affected skewed-XCI females; ceiling set by duplication carriers.  
Domains: None — no change to the coding sequence or to any domain.  
Rescue: None found for CDKL5. GeneReviews notes pathogenic variants in a 170-nt noncoding 3' region of the brain transcript, confirming the 3' end is functionally regulatory, but no ASO upregulation data exist. Searches: web 'CDKL5 3'UTR ASO upregulation', PubMed CDKL5 isoform/3'UTR.

**P4 — no exon skipped as therapy; ASO/ExSpeU1 restoration of canonical exon inclusion for a splice-site allele (e.g. c.2376+5G>A, which causes exon 16 skipping and NMD)** (likely functional) — Strongest direct CDKL5 rescue data of any option and restores the true canonical protein, but it only serves the small splice-variant subgroup and the modality demonstrated was U1 snRNA, not an ASO.  
Natural: Product is the canonical full-length protein restored from the patient's own mutant allele. Untreated equivalent: carriers of c.2376+1/+5 variants, who have classic CDD with ~80% reduction in CDKL5 protein in lymphocytes.  
Domains: None in the rescued transcript — exon 16 inclusion is restored, avoiding the frameshift and premature stop in exon 18; kinase domain 13-297 and C-terminal region intact.  
Rescue: Direct and quantitative: ExSpeU1 constructs restored >70% CDKL5 protein for the +5 model with dose-dependent recovery of kinase activity, in HEK293T, SH-SY5Y, patient lymphoblastoid cells (c.2376+5G>A) and Cdkl5-null hippocampal neurons. Critically, only +5 variants were rescuable; +1 canonical-site variants were not.

**P5 — no exon skipped; Xist-targeting ASO (± DNMT inhibitor) to reactivate the wild-type CDKL5 allele on the inactive X in females** (uncertain) — Ideal product and an exact natural-human proof of concept in skewed females, but genome-wide Xi reactivation is non-selective, the CDKL5-specific evidence is an unretrievable preprint, and it excludes males.  
Natural: Product is canonical CDKL5 from the silenced wild-type allele. Human equivalent is precisely the favourably-skewed heterozygous female, who may be mildly affected or unaffected — the best natural evidence that partial canonical restoration is clinically meaningful. Not applicable to hemizygous males.  
Domains: None — wild-type coding sequence, all domains intact.  
Rescue: CDKL5-specific: an unreviewed 2025 preprint titled 'Epigenetic Reactivation of CDKL5 Rescues CDKL5 Deficiency Disorder' exists; full text could not be retrieved, so no numbers are claimed. Cross-target precedent only: an Xist ASO plus a DNA-methylation inhibitor achieved 30,000-fold MECP2 upregulation from Xi in cultured cells with selective Xi reactivation in vivo.

**P3 — modulation of the in-frame 41-aa cassette exon 16b/exon 17 (hCDKL5_2, ~10% of brain transcripts)** (likely poor) — The exon is in-frame and not NMD-coupled, so redirecting it reshuffles isoform identity without increasing total productive CDKL5 output — it does not address the dosage deficit.  
Natural: Both resulting proteins occur naturally in human brain: the canonical 960-aa form and the exon-16b-containing form with 41 extra in-frame residues in the C-terminal half. Both are present in unaffected individuals; no phenotype is attached to the isoform ratio.  
Domains: Insertion/removal of 41 in-frame residues in the C-terminal regulatory half, downstream of the kinase domain (13-297). No domain is destroyed or fused out of frame.  
Rescue: None found. No study has modulated exon 16b with an oligonucleotide or shown that the ratio affects CDKL5 activity. Searches: PubMed 'CDKL5 exon 16b', web '"CDKL5" "exon 16b" splicing isoform'.

**P7 — COMPARATOR (larger change): a protein truncated in the distal C-terminus, i.e. terminating after aa 781, the product an ASO would yield if it bypassed a late premature stop** (uncertain) — Human data show this truncated protein confers a measurably milder motor/communication course, but these individuals still have CDD with refractory epilepsy — it is a partial product, not a normal one, and one retrieved source argues the opposite severity direction.  
Natural: Directly observed and well characterised: the 'late truncating (after aa781)' CDD variant group. These individuals retain kinase activity and most of the C-terminal region and attain milestones more often — HR 1.72 (95% CI 1.00-2.95) for independent sitting and HR 2.73 (95% CI 1.17-6.35) for independent walking versus other groups. p.Arg970* in the last exon has been reported with late-onset, milder seizure disorder, and variation beyond codon 938 has been argued to be of minor or no significance.  
Domains: Kinase domain 13-297, ATP site and catalytic D135 fully retained; the distal C-terminus (roughly aa 782 to the 960-aa brain terminus) is lost, removing the C-terminal nuclear localisation/export region that controls CDKL5 subcellular distribution.  
Rescue: No engineered or therapeutic rescue with this product found. Evidence is human natural-history only (milestone hazard ratios above). Searches: PubMed 'CDKL5 C-terminal deletion kinase activity localization' (0 hits), web late-truncating genotype-phenotype.

**P8 — COMPARATOR (largest change): a kinase-domain-only product truncated between aa 172 and aa 781, losing the whole C-terminal region** (likely poor) — Catalytic competence without the C-terminal regulatory region is exactly the configuration seen in severely affected patients, so frame restoration here would not be product quality.  
Natural: Observed as the aa172-781 truncating variant group: nonsense/frameshift alleles that preserve kinase activity but delete the C-terminal region. This group is associated with reduced likelihood of independent sitting and walking — i.e. classic severe CDD.  
Domains: Kinase domain 13-297 retained but the entire C-terminal regulatory region (~aa 298/782 onward to 960), including the NLS/NES sequences governing nuclear-cytoplasmic distribution, is removed.  
Rescue: None — this is the disease state, not a rescue. No study shows benefit of expressing a C-terminally truncated CDKL5.

**P6 — no exon skipped; allele-selective gapmer knockdown of a mutant CDKL5 transcript, wild-type spared** (likely poor) — With no named dominant-negative allele, silencing an already non-functional transcript adds no CDKL5 protein and cannot correct a dosage deficit.  
Natural: None found. CDD is established as a loss-of-function/haploinsufficiency disorder; no dominant-negative CDKL5 allele was named in this brief, and no retrieved source documents a truncated CDKL5 protein poisoning wild-type function. GeneReviews notes gain-of-function missense and duplications give a different, often milder presentation — not a degradation target.  
Domains: Not applicable — no protein product is generated by this strategy; the therapeutic output is simply unchanged wild-type protein from the other allele.  
Rescue: None found. Searches: PubMed/web CDKL5 dominant negative, allele-selective knockdown. No CDKL5 allele-selective ASO reported.

Ranking: P2 > P1 > P4 > P5 > P9 > P7 > P3 > P8 > P6. P1/P2 yield the unmodified canonical 960-aa brain protein from a healthy allele — the only outcomes with no domain cost — and P2's non-productive event is documented in human brain RNA-seq, not just a data-free patent. P4 has the only quantitative CDKL5 rescue but serves one variant subgroup. Truncated products (P7/P8) remain disease proteins.

## ASO design evidence
**P1 — Suppress inclusion of the intron-16 brain-specific alternative exon (Fichou 'exon 16b' = Williamson 'exon 17'; claimed by Stoke as 'exon 16x of CDKL5' in 'intron 16 of CDKL5'). Product: canonical hCDKL5_1 mRNA. NOT an exon-skipping/reframing block — there is no frame to restore; goal is productive-isoform share.**  
Published: None found. PubMed 'CDKL5 antisense oligonucleotide' returns 6 records, none targeting a CDKL5 exon; 'CDKL5 poison exon antisense oligonucleotide' = 0 hits. Only patent-level claim: WO2019084050A1 names 'exon 16x of CDKL5' / 'intron 16 of CDKL5' with no CDKL5 SEQ IDs; sibling filings CA3079729A1 and CN111936163A do not mention CDKL5 at all. A web result asserting ASOs 'H16A(-12+19)/H16A(-06+25) induced CDKL5 exon 16 skipping' is a false conflation — those are DMD exon-16 PMOs from the Wilton exon-skipping patents; do not use.  
Coordinated: No evidence for CDKL5. Searches run: PubMed 'CDKL5 antisense oligonucleotide', 'CDKL5 transcript isoforms alternative splicing brain exon', 'CDKL5 exon 16b isoform brain truncated', 'CDKL5 alternative splicing non-productive isoform exon 16 exon 17' (0 hits); web 'CDKL5 intron 16 pseudoexon/NMD exon/poison exon'; Europe PMC MCP returned HTTP 503 on all attempts (CDKL5 poison exon; CDKL5 splice switching) — that channel is unverified. Generic precedent only: one AO against DMD exon 17 also skipped exon 18, and co-skipping of a stretch is achievable by targeting the two outer exons.  
Linked: Yes, generic: NS-089/NCNP-02 (brogidirsen) is a single PMO built from two connected antisense sequences hitting two separate splicing regulators in DMD exon 44 — directly applicable architecture if one arm hits the 3'ss and one the ESE of the intron-16 exon. Also multi-cassette U7 snRNA vectors targeting >1 splicing event per AAV.  
Amenability: Weak as a standalone. Exon-17(=16b)-containing transcripts are ~10% of whole-brain CDKL5 transcripts, so complete suppression redistributes at most ~10% — and Williamson assigns this exon +41 in-frame amino acids (hCDKL5_2/_4), i.e. productive, not NMD-coupled. Exon is extremely conserved and brain-restricted (suggesting function, and a cryptic-regulator risk). No CDKL5 poison exon was recovered by a 2025 transcriptome-wide brain poison-exon atlas (12,014 putative PEs; CDKL5 absent), so the patent's NIE designation is unvalidated.

**P2 — Block the cryptic 5' splice donor inside exon 11 to abolish hCDKL5_3/_4 and restore full-length exon 11 coding sequence. Product: canonical hCDKL5_1.**  
Published: None found (same search set as P1; no ASO reported against CDKL5 exon 11 or its cryptic donor).  
Coordinated: Not applicable/none found — the event is an intra-exonic cryptic donor, not an adjacent-exon pair. Searches for CDKL5 multi-exon or co-skipping events (PubMed + web, listed in P1) returned nothing.  
Linked: Dual-sequence single-oligo precedent (brogidirsen) is the relevant architecture for simultaneously masking the cryptic donor and its enhancer; none reported for CDKL5.  
Amenability: Very weak ceiling: alternative exon-11 splicing accounts for <5% of brain CDKL5 transcripts, so maximal recoverable productive mRNA is <5%. Cryptic-donor masking is mechanistically the easiest ASO action (direct steric block of U1), but the payoff is below plausible therapeutic relevance alone.

**P3 — Suppress the hCDKL5_5 (CDKL5_115, testis-type) distal exon/3'-terminal configuration in favour of hCDKL5_1. Product: canonical brain mRNA.**  
Published: None found (searches as P1; also PubMed 'CDKL5 exon 16b isoform brain truncated 107 amino acids' = 0 hits).  
Coordinated: None found. No report of a single oligo altering more than one CDKL5 terminal-exon choice; searches listed in P1 plus web 'CDKL5 exon 16b hCDKL5_5 brain-specific isoform' returned no coordinated-skipping data.  
Linked: Terminal-exon switching generally needs combined polyA-signal + 3'ss masking → a two-arm single oligo (brogidirsen-type) or dual-cassette U7; no CDKL5 precedent.  
Amenability: Essentially non-amenable in the target tissue: hCDKL5_5 is detected almost exclusively in adult testis (present in fetal brain). Post-natal CNS gain would be near zero. Deprioritise.

**P4 — Redirect 5' end selection among alternative first exons 1a–1e / 5'UTR to the productive hCDKL5_1 5'UTR, or de-repress translation/transcription at the locus (regRNA/eRNA-antagonist ASO). Product: more productive full-length mRNA, coding sequence unchanged.**  
Published: None found against CDKL5 5'UTR or its regulatory RNAs. No CAMP4 CDKL5 program retrieved: their disclosed clinical assets are CMP-002 (SYNGAP1, first-in-human cleared 2026) and CMP-CPS-001 (CPS1). A secondary conference-report claim that a CDKL5 ASO is in tox at CAMP4 could not be corroborated on company or investor pages.  
Coordinated: Not applicable (no exon block). No CDKL5 coordinated-skipping report exists; searches listed in P1.  
Linked: regRNA-antagonist ASOs are single-site; the precedent that matters is platform-level (allele-agnostic upregulation ASO entering humans for a DEE gene), not architecture.  
Amenability: Unquantified for CDKL5: five alternative 5'UTR exons (1a–1e) are annotated with no published usage fractions, and no CDKL5 regRNA map is published. Attractive because it is mutation-agnostic and does not touch the coding sequence, but it is the least de-risked option — requires a locus-level regRNA/TSS screen before design.

**P5 — Xist-targeting ASO (± DNMT inhibitor) to reactivate the wild-type CDKL5 allele on the inactive X in female patients. Product: additional full-length CDKL5 mRNA from Xi; no splicing change.**  
Published: Xist ASO reported (Carrette et al.), combinatorial with 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine; sequences not extracted here. No CDKL5-specific oligo. This is the only retrieved ASO precedent that explicitly names CDKL5 syndrome as an intended indication.  
Coordinated: Not applicable — mechanism is chromatin/lncRNA, not splicing. No CDKL5 coordinated-skipping evidence exists (searches in P1).  
Linked: Precedent is 'mixed modality' (ASO + small molecule) rather than a linked oligo — a genuine multi-agent architecture with in vivo proof of concept.  
Amenability: Mechanistically target-agnostic, so CDKL5-specific amenability is unknown; applies only to heterozygous females (not hemizygous males) and depends on XCI mosaicism per neuron. Chief liability is non-selectivity: genome-wide Xi reactivation plus DNMT inhibition. Reported as achieving selective Xi reactivation without evident toxicity in a brain-specific genetic Xist-ablation model.

**P6 — Variant-dependent fallback: splice-correcting oligo for CDKL5 +5 splice-site variants (c.99+5G>A exon 3 GC-donor; c.2376+5G>A exon 16 donor). Product: restored correctly spliced full-length mRNA from the mutant allele. Requires a named variant — none supplied in this brief.**  
Published: No ASOs found. Equivalent correction demonstrated with engineered U1 snRNA (ExSpeU1), not oligonucleotides: >70% CDKL5 protein rescue for c.99+5G>A, dose-dependent, with restored subcellular distribution, TEY phosphorylation and dendritic morphology in primary neurons. ExSpeU1 rescued +5 variants but failed at +1 variants.  
Coordinated: Not applicable. Note the inverse phenomenon is documented: c.2376+5G>A causes exon 16 skipping → frameshift → premature stop in exon 18 → NMD, i.e. this exon block must NOT be skipped.  
Linked: Combined engineered-U1 + ASO treatment of a single splice-site lesion has precedent in BBS1; relevant if one arm must mask a cryptic site while the other strengthens the weakened donor.  
Amenability: Exon 16's donor is demonstrably fragile — a +5 substitution alone removes ~80% of correctly spliced transcript and ~80% of CDKL5 protein in patient lymphocytes, and exon 3 uses a non-canonical GC 5'ss. That fragility is a warning for P1: an ASO walking intron 16 risks destabilising exon 16 definition and producing the NMD-coupled exon-16-skipped species.

Non-transferable benchmarks: (1) STK-001/zorevunersen (SCN1A poison exon 20x): seizure reduction plus cognition/behaviour gains in Ph1/2a. Non-transferable — SCN1A has a validated, highly included NMD exon; CDKL5 has no validated poison exon (absent from a 2025 transcriptome-wide brain PE atlas), and its only quantified alternative exon is in-frame/productive at ~10% usage. The SCN1A productive-mRNA headroom simply does not exist here. (2) SYNGAP1 poison-exon SSO (NMD transcript down, protein up in patient neurons) and CAMP4 CMP-002 — platform/clinical-feasibility precedent for allele-agnostic CNS upregulation only, no CDKL5 potency implication. (3) Carrette's 30,000-fold MECP2 upregulation from Xi — a cultured-cell, mixed-modality (ASO + 5-aza) number; it is not an in vivo CDKL5 expression expectation. (4) Brogidirsen/NS-089 exon-44 skipping percentages in DMD muscle and monkey — architecture precedent for a two-arm single oligo only; DMD skipping efficiency says nothing about CDKL5 isoform redistribution or CNS exposure. (5) ExSpeU1 '>70% rescue' for CDKL5 c.99+5G>A is AAV/U1-delivered and variant-specific; it is not an ASO benchmark and does not apply to the mutation-agnostic products. (6) Nusinersen-class intrathecal CNS potency should be read as delivery/dosing precedent, not payload potency, since SMN2 exon 7 inclusion has an exceptionally favourable baseline.

Proposed designs:
- Prerequisite screen (not a molecule): long-read + NMD-inhibition RNA-seq of human neurons across CDKL5 introns to test whether patent-claimed intron-16 'exon 16x' is PTC-introducing; no design without it.
- P1 lead: 2'-MOE PS 18-20mers tiling the intron-16 alternative exon 3'ss, branch point and internal ESE; ~40 oligos at 5-nt steps; readout productive/total CDKL5 by ddPCR.
- P1 optimisation: single two-arm PMO/MOE oligo (brogidirsen architecture) joining the best 3'ss-blocking and best ESE-blocking sequences, to exceed the ~10% redistribution ceiling.
- Counter-screen in every design: RT-PCR for exon-16-skipped NMD species, since exon 16's donor loses ~80% of correct splicing to a single +5 change.

## Delivery platforms
**Unconjugated 2'-MOE phosphorothioate steric-block ASO, intrathecal bolus (baseline platform for a CDKL5 upregulator)** — Fully 2'-MOE PS, 18-20mer, no RNase H recruitment (splice-switching/TANGO-type); no ligand. Free uptake into CSF-exposed cells; dosed by lumbar puncture, pediatric-feasible.  
Reach: Yes for CSF-adjacent CNS, with a steep gradient. Human autopsy/imaging: caudal-to-rostral gradient, highest lumbar cord, most intense at cortical surface with little diffusion into deeper brain; NHP Malat1 ASO accumulation >1 order of magnitude lower in deep brain than cortex/cord. Brainstem, hippocampus, cerebellar deep layers and GABAergic interneuron populations in deep structures are the weak compartments. Retina is NOT reliably reached intrathecally (separate intravitreal route needed). Heart/periphery exposure low after IT (safety advantage).  
Human PD: Molecular CNS PD for upregulation ASOs is indirect (no CSF protein assay). Zorevunersen (SCN1A TANGO, intrathecal): OLE median major-motor-seizure reduction ≥50% at all timepoints, 87% median at 8 months (n=9, 70 mg x2-3 then 45 mg); Vineland-3 expressive +7.56, receptive +6.06 points over 2 yr; ~95% rated improved at 36 mo. ION582/obudanersen (UBE3A-ATS, intrathecal, n=51): ~65% improved on Bayley-4 motor at 6 mo. Mechanistic/RNase H benchmark for CNS oligo exposure: intrathecal mivelsiran (siRNA) 75 mg single dose, CSF sAPPβ −90% max, −39% at 10 mo.  
Dose: Loading then every 4 months intrathecal (zorevunersen: two 70 mg loading doses, 45 mg maintenance q4mo). Nusinersen-type schedule q4mo is the accepted pediatric standard.  
Tox: Class: post-LP syndrome, CSF protein elevation (zorevunersen ~94% of patients with elevated CSF protein, 59% logged as TEAE; no hydrocephalus, no serious clinical sequelae reported), thrombocytopenia/proteinuria monitoring, tofersen-class myelitis/radiculitis. No serious safety events reported for STK-002 to date; ION582 'safe and well tolerated at all doses'.  
Access: Fully buildable internally. Chemistry is generic/off-patent-adjacent; the constraint is IP on TANGO-style non-productive-splicing claims (Stoke) if the design reads on a poison/NMD-exon mechanism. Stoke's own disclosed CNS pipeline is SCN1A and SYNGAP1 — no CDKL5 program found. Routes: internal build; partner Stoke/Ionis; or the individualised-ASO platform trial route (NCT07410143) for variant-specific cases.

**Unconjugated intravitreal ASO (retinal compartment)** — Same 2'-MOE steric-block chemistry, intravitreal injection into one/both eyes; separate dosing compartment from intrathecal.  
Reach: Retinal ganglion cells and inner retina after IVT; does not require BBB crossing. Does not address brain — a CDKL5 program needing retina would run IT + IVT as two routes.  
Human PD: None quantitative retrieved. OSPREY Phase 1/2 first cohort dosed, second cohort from Aug 2026; safety/efficacy readout H1 2027; no serious or severe safety events reported so far.  
Dose: Intravitreal, one eye, intermittent (interval not disclosed in retrieved sources).  
Tox: No serious or severe safety events reported to date in STK-002 studies; class risk is IVT procedure (endophthalmitis, IOP) plus ASO-class intraocular inflammation.  
Access: Internal build straightforward; same IP caveat as above. Retina should be treated as optional/secondary for CDD — deprioritize unless visual phenotype is the endpoint.

**TfR1 antibody/Fab-oligonucleotide conjugates (Denali OTV, Roche Brainshuttle-ASO, Dyne FORCE, Avidity/Novartis AOC)** — Anti-TfR1 antibody or Fab/VHH conjugated to oligo (e.g. maleimide-C6 linker to oligo 5' at engineered Ser239 in Denali OTV); IV/SC dosing, receptor-mediated transcytosis.  
Reach: Best current answer to the deep-brain/interneuron gap: preclinical OTV gives sustained knockdown across multiple CNS regions and all major cell types (neurons, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocytes, endothelium). Dyne TfR1-Fab-siMAPT ~75% brain MAPT knockdown in mice and NHP including deep brain; TfR1 VHH-siRNA 50-80% durable knockdown in mice/NHP. Systemic route means heart/liver/muscle exposure and TfR1-mediated reticulocyte/ hematologic risk must be managed.  
Human PD: None found for CNS. DNL628 (OTV:MAPT) first patient dosed March 2026 in Phase 1b Alzheimer's; initial clinical biomarker data expected H1 2027. DNL422 (OTV:SNCA) IND-enabling. Human AOC PD exists only for muscle (del-siran DM1, Phase 3 readout Sept 2026).  
Dose: IV/SC, expected monthly-to-quarterly; not yet defined in humans for CNS.  
Tox: Unknown in human CNS. Class concerns: TfR1-driven anemia/reticulocyte loss, infusion reactions, systemic oligo exposure (liver/kidney), plus ADA. Published mAb work explicitly frames the brain-exposure/PK/safety tradeoff for TfR antibodies.  
Access: Not internally buildable at credible speed. All leading TfR-oligo assets are captive (Denali, Roche, Dyne, Novartis post-Avidity). Realistic paths: license a TfR shuttle from a tools/biotech provider (VHH-based academic/biotech constructs are the most licensable), or partner. Treat as generation-2 for CDD, not the lead.

**Peptide-conjugated oligonucleotides for systemic CNS delivery (ApoB11/LDLR, ApoE-derived, CPP/EEV classes)** — Short receptor-binding peptide (e.g. 11-aa ApoB-derived LDLR ligand) covalently conjugated to ASO; systemic (IP/IV) dosing.  
Reach: Rodent-only evidence of brain penetration with sustained retention; no NHP or human regional/cell-type mapping retrieved. Cell-type coverage in interneurons/cerebellum unknown.  
Human PD: None found. No clinical-stage CNS peptide-oligo conjugate retrieved; PepGen/Entrada EEV CNS data did not surface in searches (searches run: 'peptide conjugated oligonucleotide brain delivery 2026 PepGen Entrada EEV CNS antisense').  
Dose: Not established; rodent single-dose PK only (ApoB11:ASO brain Cmax 1.5 h, high levels to 96 h).  
Tox: Rodent tolerability reported; historic CPP class liabilities are renal tubular toxicity and complement/infusion effects at efficacious systemic doses. No human data.  
Access: Academic/early-biotech licensing is feasible and cheap, but the platform is too immature to carry a CDD lead. Use as an option-value side bet only.

**Adjacent/newer classes relevant as precedent or competition (intrathecal conjugated siRNA; AAV gene replacement; AAV-U1 snRNA)** — C16-conjugated siRNA intrathecal (mivelsiran); AAV9 CDKL5 gene replacement (ICV/IT); engineered U1 snRNA delivered by vector.  
Reach: Intrathecal C16-siRNA achieves durable human CNS target engagement (same compartment limits as IT ASO). AAV9 ICV in neonatal Cdkl5-KO mice reduced pathology and aberrant behavior; NHP data indicate CDKL5-expressing neurons tolerate added CDKL5 — but AAV9 CNS coverage in humans is patchy and one-shot, non-titratable.  
Human PD: Mivelsiran 75 mg single intrathecal dose: CSF sAPPα −84%, sAPPβ −90% max; −33%/−39% at 10 months; first RNAi gene silencing demonstrated in human brain. AAV CDKL5: no human data — UX055 remains preclinical with no timelines; Elaaj Bio (Loulou subsidiary) partnered with Viralgen for manufacturing.  
Dose: Mivelsiran: single IT dose with ~12-month durability (supports very infrequent CNS oligo dosing). AAV: single dose, irreversible.  
Tox: Mivelsiran: mostly mild/moderate AEs, no significant CSF-biomarker or NfL abnormality. AAV: immunogenicity, no redosing, dose-finding unresolved (explicit reason for UX055 delay).  
Access: Mivelsiran chemistry (lipid-conjugated IT oligo, Alnylam) is licensing-only. AAV route is already occupied in CDD by Ultragenyx and Elaaj Bio — relevant as competition, and as the reason an ASO must offer titratability/reversibility to differentiate.

Unconjugated baseline: Unconjugated 2'-MOE PS steric-block ASO, intrathecal bolus, is the only buildable route today for a CDKL5-upregulating ASO. Dosing template: two 70 mg loading doses then 45 mg every 4 months by LP (zorevunersen); nusinersen-type loading+q4mo is the accepted pediatric schedule. Delivered performance in CSF-accessible CNS: clinically meaningful, e.g. zorevunersen OLE median major-motor-seizure reduction 87% at 8 months (n=9) with Vineland-3 expressive +7.56/receptive +6.06 over 2 years; ION582 ~65% of 51 patients improved on Bayley-4 motor at 6 months. Exposure limits are quantified and material: NHP deep-brain ASO accumulation >10-fold below cortex/cord; human nusinersen autopsy shows a caudal-to-rostral gradient with little drug in brainstem/cortex; tofersen imaging shows cortical-surface-dominant signal, brain ~4-10% of injected dose. Tolerability at this exposure is manageable but not clean: CSF protein elevation in ~94% of zorevunersen patients (59% TEAE), no hydrocephalus; no serious events reported for ION582 or STK-002. Retina needs a second, intravitreal route (STK-002 precedent). No approved or clinical unconjugated oligo reaches CNS after systemic dosing.

Payload vs delivery: Zorevunersen's 87% seizure drop and ION582's Bayley gains reflect target amenability — a validated SCN1A poison exon, a UBE3A-ATS switch — plus open-label design, not superior delivery; the delivery vehicle is identical plain IT 2'-MOE. For CDKL5 the binding risk is payload: no validated CDKL5 non-productive/NMD-coupled splicing event was retrieved, and no CDKL5 ASO program exists. Deep-brain/interneuron exposure is the delivery-side deficit.
