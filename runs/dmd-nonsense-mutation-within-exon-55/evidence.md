## Candidates considered
| Role | Concept | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| REJECT | Single exon 55 skip (54|56 junction) — the brief's stated goal | Enumeration is decisive: 190 bp, frame remainder 1, so the junction frameshifts; the product terminates inside spectrin repeat 21 with no WW/ZZ/cysteine-rich or C-terminal domain and is itself an NMD substrate. The brief's own fallback condition is triggered. |
| LEAD | Double skip exons 54-55 via a single exon-54-directed PMO exploiting documented 54+55 coupling (53|56 junction, −115 aa) | Smallest in-frame block containing the lesion; the only block with published single-AO coordinated skipping of both its exons; 108-aa hybrid-repeat junction; keeps nNOS repeats and all of exons 56-79; stops at the exon-55 boundary. Coupling ratio unverified, hence TEST FIRST. |
| LEAD | Double skip exons 54-55 via two-PMO cocktail (exon-54 ESE oligo + exon-55 acceptor oligo) | Same product and the pre-specified backstop inside the lead experiment if coupling is partial; both oligos have published starting sequences. Costs a second drug substance and makes yield multiplicative. |
| BACKUP | Double skip exons 54-55 via brogidirsen-style dual-arm single molecule | Preserves a single drug substance while forcing both skips; architecturally precedented by a clinical dual-sequence PMO, but that linked design is Nippon Shinyaku IP needing freedom-to-operate review and there is no DMD multi-exon dual-arm precedent. |
| BACKUP | Double skip exons 55-56 (54|57 junction, −121 aa) | Co-equal on frame and nearly equal on size with a validated exon-56 oligo, but no coordinated skipping reported for this pair, it removes essentially all of repeat 22 leaving a 6-aa stub rather than a hybrid repeat, and it is the only two-exon option entering the exon 56-79 region where human benign deletions stop. |
| BACKUP | Four-exon skip 52-55 (−225 aa) as a 3-4 PMO cocktail | Best product-level in vivo evidence in the set — CRISPR Dmd Δ52-55 mouse with WT-level correctly localised dystrophin and normal function at 12 weeks — and possibly three oligos if exon-54 targeting carries exon 55; but it deletes 110 more residues and the same mouse shows exercise-stress decrement at 52 weeks. |
| WATCH | Eight-exon skip 48-55 (−435 aa) | Has the only genuinely asymptomatic adult human carrier in the set (35-year-old male, normal CK 198 IU/L) and retains the nNOS-binding repeats that 45-55 loses, but needs up to eight oligos — unbuildable as a lead for a single exon-55 PTC. Keep as the human-genetics reference point. |
| WATCH | Six-exon skip 50-55 (−339 aa) | Human-anchored only by containment within the benign 45-55/48-55 intervals and the late-cardiomyopathy signature of hinge-3 rod deletions; up to six oligos and an awkward oversized 145-aa junction element for no advantage over 52-55. |
| WATCH | Eleven-exon skip 45-55 as a 5-6 PMO cocktail | Unmatched human genotype-phenotype evidence (~90% of del45-55 carriers are BMD, 75/83) and the richest ASO precedent, but it deletes the most protein, loses the nNOSμ-binding site whose cytosolic mislocalisation tracks with worse phenotype, and needs 5-6 oligos — all unnecessary for a single exon-55 PTC. | REJECT | Four-exon skip 54-57 (−225 aa) | No size advantage over 52-55, deletes two extra exons inside the exon 56-79 interval human genetics flags as intolerant, gives an oversized 126-aa junction element, and has no ASO efficiency data for exons 56 or 57. |
| REJECT | Six-exon skip 52-57 (−335 aa) | Strictly dominated by 52-55: same upstream breakpoint, 110 more residues removed, and it crosses into the exon 56-79 region. |
| REJECT | Six/seven-exon skips 53-58 and 54-59 | Both remove three of the four repeats immediately upstream of the WW/cysteine-rich anchor and lie wholly inside the exon 56-79 interval where no benign human in-frame deletion exists. |
| REJECT | Partial exon 55 removal via an exon-internal cryptic splice site | Contingent on an unverified cryptic acceptor or donor at a frame-compatible distance from the PTC; nothing retrieved maps one. Worse, both published exon-55 oligos sit on the 3' splice site, the configuration that provokes cryptic use — so this is an off-target readout to exclude, not a mechanism to pursue. |
| REJECT | NMD evasion / stabilising the exon-55 PTC transcript | Yields more of a rod-domain truncate that cannot bind β-dystroglycan or anchor the DGC, and ASOs cannot induce readthrough. Drop from the program rather than carry as a backup. |
| REJECT | Output-raising mechanisms: poison-exon block, 5'UTR/uORF or 3'UTR modulation, antagoNAT transcriptional upregulation | All amplify a message that still carries the exon-55 stop codon; no DMD poison exon, repressive uORF or repressive natural antisense transcript was retrieved. Wrong axis for a nonsense lesion. |
| REJECT | Transcript or allele knockdown (RNase H gapmer, allele-selective against the SNV) | Actively harmful in a hemizygous male — there is no second allele to spare and degradation removes rather than restores function. |
| WATCH | Surrogate-gene upregulation by ASO (UTRN or disease-modifier transcripts) | Genuinely mutation-agnostic and independent of the PTC, so legitimate as a later combination arm, but no druggable repressive element in UTRN was located in any retrieval and the achievable fold-change and mechanical benefit are unestablished. Unlocated, not excluded. |
| WATCH | Nonsense-specific base editing (PBGENE-DMD, single-vector ABE) as the competing mechanism | Not an ASO option but the direct competitor for this exact lesion class, restoring full-length dystrophin in one dose; the live AAV safety crisis in DMD gene therapy and the reversibility of chronic dosing are the ASO program's counter-arguments. Monitor, do not build. |

## Products
**Single exon 55 skip (54|56 junction) — brief's stated goal, mechanism 'not applicable'** (likely poor) — Frame arithmetic from the enumeration is decisive — the product is a rod-domain truncate with no sarcolemmal anchor, not a Becker protein.  
Natural: None found as a benign allele. Single-exon out-of-frame deletions in the distal rod are DMD-type; no asymptomatic carrier of an exon-55-only deletion was found in the mild-deletion review (Fortunato 2023) or in BMD natural-history cohorts. Searches: 'exon 55 deletion DMD phenotype', 'mild dystrophinopathy deletion clusters', BMD natural history 225 patients.  
Domains: Enumeration: exon 55 = 190 bp, frame remainder 1, so the 54|56 junction is out of frame. Product is not an internally deleted protein at all — translation frameshifts just after aa ~2675 (within spectrin repeat 21, UniProt 2580-2686) and stops downstream, losing repeats 22-24, WW (3055-3088), ZZ (3308-3364), the dystroglycan-binding cysteine-rich region and the syntrophin-binding C-terminus (3466-3518).  
Rescue: None found, and none possible in principle: a frameshifted transcript is itself an NMD substrate. No paper was found reporting dystrophin rescue by exon-55-only skipping.

**Double skip exons 54-55 (2 exons, 345 bp, Δaa 2625-2739, -115; junction ACAGAAACCAAG|GACCTCCAAGGT)** (likely functional) — Smallest in-frame product, loses only ~1 repeat's worth of distal rod, keeps nNOS-binding repeats and the whole exon 56-79 region, and the junction length predicts a foldable hybrid repeat — but the human genotype evidence is indirect.  
Natural: None found. No reported human del54-55 genomic deletion with phenotype was retrieved. Searches run: 'deletion of exons 54-55 Becker patient' (extended), '"exon 54" "exon 55" DMD deletion in-frame cohort' (extended), Fortunato mild-deletion review, BMD natural-history cohort, Europe PMC (503 error, retried). Closest human anchors are deletions that end at exon 55: del48-55 (asymptomatic 35-year-old male, normal CK) and del45-55 (asymptomatic to mild BMD).  
Domains: Δ2625-2739 removes the C-terminal ~62 aa of spectrin repeat 21 (2580-2686) and the N-terminal 51 aa of repeat 22 (2689-2802). The junction fuses R21 aa 2580-2624 (45 aa) to R22 aa 2740-2802 (63 aa) = 108 aa, i.e. the length of one canonical repeat (R21 = 107 aa, R22 = 114 aa) — the structurally favourable 'hybrid repeat' configuration rather than a fractional repeat. Everything else is untouched: actin-binding CH1/CH2 (15-240), nNOSμ-binding repeats 16-17 (1992-2208), hinge 3, repeats 23-24, WW, ZZ, cysteine-rich and C-terminal syntrophin/dystrobrevin region. Deletion stays entirely upstream of exon 56, the boundary beyond which no asymptomatic in-frame deletion has been observed.  
Rescue: No direct del54-55 rescue data found. Indirect: PMO cocktails covering exons 45-55 (which include exon-54 and exon-55 oligos) skip multiple exons and restore dystrophin in DMD patient-derived myotubes; and multi-exon 45-55 skipping cocktails work systemically in mdx52 mice. Exon-54 and exon-55 skipping efficiencies in that work are tool-predicted (62.0% and 120.4%), not measured for a 54+55-only pair.

**Double skip exons 55-56 (2 exons, 363 bp, Δaa 2676-2796, -121; junction CATTCATAAAAG|GTCCCATTTGGA)** (uncertain) — Equal on frame and nearly equal on size, but it eats a whole repeat without an equal-length replacement and is the only two-exon option that breaches the exon-56 boundary where human benign deletions stop.  
Natural: None found. Searches: '"exon 55-56" in-frame deletion phenotype Becker' (extended), Fortunato review, BMD natural-history cohort. Relevant negative evidence: no asymptomatic in-frame deletion in the mild-deletion review extended beyond exon 55, and the authors argue the exon 56-79 territory is the functionally conserved core.  
Domains: Δ2676-2796 clips only the last 11 aa of repeat 21 but removes 108 of the 114 aa of repeat 22, leaving a 6-aa R22 stub; the fused element is ~102 aa, short of a canonical repeat — a fractional/frayed junction rather than a clean hybrid repeat. Repeats 23-24, CR and C-terminus intact; nNOS-binding R16-17 intact. Crosses the exon-55/56 boundary.  
Rescue: None found specific to del55-56. Exon-56 ASO skipping efficiency was not retrieved in any dataset (the 45-55 cocktail work stops at exon 55), so this block has no oligo precedent at all for one of its two exons.

**Four-exon skip 52-55 (675 bp, Δaa 2515-2739, -225; junction ATCAAGCAGAAG|GACCTCCAAGGT)** (likely functional) — This is the only candidate product with a purpose-built in vivo model showing full-level, correctly localised protein and near-normal muscle, with only a late exercise-stress decrement.  
Natural: No individually phenotyped human del52-55 case retrieved, but it sits in the exon 45-55 hotspot whose deletions are the classic mild/BMD cluster, and the equivalent mouse allele was made deliberately as a BMD model. Searches: 'deletion exons 52-55 Becker patient' (extended), Fortunato review, MLPA genotype-phenotype cohort.  
Domains: Δ2515-2739 removes the C-terminal 63 aa of repeat 20 (2475-2577), all of repeat 21, and the N-terminal half of repeat 22. Junction fuses R20 aa 2475-2514 (40 aa) to R22 aa 2740-2802 (63 aa) = 103 aa, close to one repeat length (near-hybrid). Hinge 3 (between R19 and R20, ~2424-2474), nNOS-binding R16-17, CH1/CH2, R23-R24, WW, ZZ, cysteine-rich and C-terminus all preserved; stops at the exon-55 boundary.  
Rescue: Strongest animal evidence of any candidate here: a CRISPR Dmd Δ52-55 mouse expresses WT-like levels of truncated dystrophin with normal DGC localisation at the sarcolemma, normal grip strength, <1% central nuclei, no fibrosis and no Evans-blue uptake after exercise at 12 weeks; by 52 weeks exercised mice show reduced grip strength and lower contractile force at 75-125 Hz, with cardiac structure/function largely preserved through 52 weeks. No ASO-induced 52-55 skipping dataset found; exon 52 and 53 PMOs exist within the 45-55 cocktails.

**Four-exon skip 54-57 (675 bp, Δaa 2625-2849, -225; junction ACAGAAACCAAG|GCCTTCAAGAGG)** (uncertain) — Frame-legal and domain-sparing on paper, but it deletes 225 aa including two exons of the exon 56-79 region where no human benign deletion exists, for no size advantage over 52-55.  
Natural: None found; no benign human deletion extending past exon 55 was identified in the mild-deletion review (only an exon-78 outlier). Searches as above plus 'deletion exons 54-57 DMD'.  
Domains: Δ2625-2849 removes the C-terminal half of repeat 21, all of repeat 22 and the N-terminal 42 aa of repeat 23 (2808-2930); the fused element is R21 aa 2580-2624 (45 aa) + R23 aa 2850-2930 (81 aa) = 126 aa, oversized relative to a canonical repeat. CR/C-terminal domains and nNOS-binding repeats intact.  
Rescue: None found for this block; no exon-56 or exon-57 ASO efficiency data retrieved.

**Six-exon skip 50-55 (1017 bp, Δaa 2401-2739, -339; junction CAGCCAGTGAAG|GACCTCCAAGGT)** (plausible) — Human-anchored by containment within the benign 45-55/48-55 intervals and by the late-cardiomyopathy signature of hinge-3 rod deletions, but it costs up to 6 oligos and gives an awkward 145-aa junction element.  
Natural: No isolated del50-55 case retrieved, but it is a sub-interval of the del45-55 and del48-55 alleles that are documented asymptomatic/mild, and it removes hinge 3 exactly as those do. Searches: 'deletion exons 50-55 Becker phenotype' (extended), Fortunato review.  
Domains: Δ2401-2739 removes the C-terminal 23 aa of repeat 19 (2319-2423), the entire inter-repeat/hinge-3 linker (~2424-2474), all of repeat 20, all of repeat 21 and the N-half of repeat 22; junction fuses R19 aa 2319-2400 (82 aa) to R22 aa 2740-2802 (63 aa) = 145 aa, an oversized non-canonical element. nNOS-binding R16-17 (1992-2208), actin-binding CH domains, R23-24, CR and C-terminus preserved.  
Rescue: No del50-55-specific rescue found. Indirect: hinge-3-removing rod deletions are the latest-onset cardiomyopathy group in BMD (mid-40s versus mid-20s for N-terminal deletions), and exon-50 PMOs are part of validated 45-55 cocktails.

**Six-exon skip 52-57 (1005 bp, Δaa 2515-2849, -335)** (uncertain) — Strictly worse than 52-55 — same upstream breakpoint, 110 more residues deleted, and it enters the exon 56-79 region.  
Natural: None found; extends two exons past the exon-55 boundary beyond which no asymptomatic deletion was observed.  
Domains: Removes R20 C-half, all R21, all R22 and the N-terminal 42 aa of R23; junction fuses 40 aa of R20 to 81 aa of R23 = 121 aa. CR/C-terminus and nNOS-binding repeats intact.  
Rescue: None found.

**Six-exon skip 53-58 (1008 bp, Δaa 2554-2889, -336) and seven/eight-exon skip 54-59 (Δaa 2625-2979, -355)** (likely poor) — They delete three of the four repeats that sit immediately upstream of the WW/cysteine-rich anchor and lie wholly inside the exon 56-79 interval that human genetics flags as intolerant.  
Natural: None found for either; both breach exon 56 and reach exons 58-59.  
Domains: 53-58: removes the C-terminal 24 aa of R20, all R21-R22 and most of R23, fusing R20 aa 2475-2553 (79 aa) to R23 aa 2890-2930 (41 aa) = 120 aa. 54-59: removes R21 C-half, all R22, all R23 and the N-terminal 45 aa of R24 (2935-3040), fusing 45 aa of R21 to R24 aa 2980-3040 (61 aa) = 106 aa, i.e. hybrid-length but at the cost of ~3 repeats and the loss of most of the distal rod immediately preceding WW/CR.  
Rescue: None found.

**Eight-exon skip 48-55 (1305 bp, Δaa 2305-2739, -435; junction CAGTGGATAAAG|GACCTCCAAGGT)** (likely functional) — It is the only product in this set with a documented, genuinely asymptomatic adult human carrier, and it keeps the nNOS-binding repeats that del45-55 loses — the problem is purely buildability (up to 8 oligos).  
Natural: Best single human anchor in the whole set: a 35-year-old male carrying a DMD exons 48-55 deletion was completely asymptomatic — normal muscle strength, no pseudohypertrophy, normal serum CK at 198 IU/L (ref 20-270) — found incidentally during prenatal testing of his daughter. Caveat: a single case at 35 years, with the authors noting follow-up is needed.  
Domains: Δ2305-2739 removes the C-terminal ~14 aa of repeat 18 (2211-2318), all of repeat 19, hinge 3, all of repeat 20, all of repeat 21 and the N-half of repeat 22; the junction fuses R18 aa 2211-2304 (94 aa) to R22 aa 2740-2802 (63 aa) = 157 aa. Critically it stops short of repeats 16-17 (1992-2208), which carry the nNOSμ-binding site, so unlike del45-55 the nNOS site is retained; CH1/CH2, R23-24, WW, ZZ, CR and C-terminus intact.  
Rescue: No ASO-induced 48-55 skipping dataset found. Exon-48 through exon-55 PMOs individually exist within published 45-55 cocktails, which restored dystrophin in DMD patient-derived myotubes.

**Eleven-exon skip 45-55 (the published multi-exon hotspot block; in-frame per literature — exact aa range not in the supplied enumeration, which lists only the 16 smallest of 31 blocks)** (likely functional) — Human genotype-phenotype evidence is unmatched and ASO feasibility is published, but it deletes the most protein, loses nNOS anchoring and needs 5-6 oligos — all unnecessary when the lesion is a single exon-55 PTC.  
Natural: The best-characterised human equivalent of any DMD skipping product: del45-55 carriers range from asymptomatic (including a 69-year-old asymptomatic grandfather and two further asymptomatic boys in one family) to mild late-onset BMD; it was the most frequent asymptomatic in-frame deletion (N=6) in the mild-deletion review and the most frequent deletion (17/68) in one cohort; in a 225-patient BMD natural-history study, del45-55 patients did not require ventilation. But phenotype is variable: in 12 biopsied del45-55 BMD patients nNOSμ expression/localisation varied widely and strictly cytosolic nNOSμ tracked with the more severe phenotypes.  
Domains: Removes the nNOSμ-binding spectrin repeats 16-17 region (aa 1992-2208; nNOS site encoded by exons 42-45) together with R18-R22 proximal sequence and hinge 3. Actin-binding CH1/CH2, R23-R24, WW, ZZ, cysteine-rich and C-terminal syntrophin/dystrobrevin domains intact. This is the one candidate that sacrifices the nNOS site that all the smaller blocks keep.  
Rescue: The most developed ASO precedent: PMO cocktails (5-6 oligos, mutation-tailored) skip exons 45-55 and restore dystrophin in DMD patient fibroblast-derived myotubes and in immortalised DMD muscle cells; a 45-55 cocktail has been delivered systemically and evaluated in mdx52 mice. No clinical-stage 45-55 cocktail program was found.

**Non-skipping mechanisms whose product is a truncated/altered protein: NMD evasion (stabilising the exon-55 PTC transcript), 5'UTR/3'UTR or transcriptional upregulation, and intra-exonic cryptic-site partial exon 55 removal** (likely poor) — More of a protein that cannot bind β-dystroglycan is not therapy, and the cryptic-site route depends on a site nobody has shown exists at a frame-compatible position.  
Natural: For NMD evasion/upregulation the product equals the natural rod-domain truncate seen in DMD nonsense patients — uniformly severe DMD, not Becker; no benign human equivalent found. For partial-exon-55 removal, no human allele with an intra-exon-55 internal deletion was retrieved.  
Domains: NMD evasion/upregulation: protein stops within repeat 21 (2580-2686), losing repeats 22-24, WW (3055-3088), ZZ (3308-3364), the β-dystroglycan-binding cysteine-rich region and the syntrophin-binding C-terminus (3466-3518) — no DGC anchoring. Cryptic-site partial exon 55 removal: would delete an unknown sub-segment of repeat 21/22; therapeutic only if the retained fragment keeps the frame, which is unverified.  
Rescue: None found. No ASO-mediated readthrough or NMD-evasion rescue of a DMD nonsense allele producing functional dystrophin was retrieved; readthrough is a small-molecule mechanism, not an oligo one.

Ranking: Rank: 54-55 > 52-55 > 48-55 > 50-55 > 45-55 > 55-56 ≈ 54-57 > 52-57 > 53-58/54-59; single exon-55 skip and NMD/upregulation routes are dead. 54-55 is smallest, keeps nNOS repeats and all of exons 56-79, and its 108-aa junction predicts a foldable hybrid repeat; 52-55 adds a BMD mouse showing WT-level, correctly localised dystrophin; 48-55 has the one asymptomatic human carrier.

## ASO design evidence
**Single exon 55 skip (brief's stated goal; frame-invalid per enumeration)**  
Published: Yes. Wilton et al. 2007 built AOs against every human DMD exon except the first and last, and every targeted exon could be removed (PMID 17285139). Specific exon-55 oligos retrieved: H55A(-10+20), a 2'-O-methyl AO annealing to the last 10 nt of intron 54 + first 20 nt of exon 55, reported to give dose-dependent exon 55 skipping (US9758783B2); and the PMO Ex55_Ac0 30-mer TCTTCCAAAGCAGCCTCTCGCTCACTCACC, sitting on the exon 55 acceptor, used in the Yokota-lab 45-55 cocktails (PMC6838919, PMC5957359).  
Target evidence: n/a (canonical exon, splice sites intact). Enumeration is decisive: exon 55 = 190 bp, frame remainder 1, so the 54|56 junction is out of frame.  
Coordinated: Relevant in reverse: an AO directed to exon 54 removes exons 54+55 together, so a 54-directed oligo is a better route to the in-frame product than an exon-55 oligo. No report found of an exon-55-directed oligo also removing 54 or 56. Searches: PubMed 'DMD exon 55 antisense oligonucleotide skipping', 'double exon skipping single antisense oligonucleotide dystrophin two adjacent exons' (0 hits); web 'single antisense oligonucleotide induced skipping of two adjacent dystrophin exons'; Europe PMC unavailable (HTTP 503 on three attempts).  
Linked: Brogidirsen (NS-089/NCNP-02) is a single PMO molecule containing two linked sequences against two separate sites in exon 44 — direct precedent for putting two binding arms in one oligo (PMID 40725431, NCT05996003).  
Amenability: Exon 55 is amenable at the acceptor site (H55A(-10+20) and Ex55_Ac0 both target -10/0 relative to the 3' splice site). Algorithmic skipping-efficiency score for Ex55_Ac0 was 120.4% — a software prediction, not a measurement; the measured cell-based readout reported was simply >20% individual skipping at 5-10 uM. No endogenous single exon-55 skipping was detected in the one study that systematically looked for endogenous skipping around this hotspot (PMC5085753).

**Double skip of exons 54-55 (lead: 345 bp, -115 aa, 53|56 junction)**  
Published: Exon 54: H54A(+67+97), reported to give strong and consistent exon 54 skipping (US9758783B2); PMO Ex54_Ac42 30-mer GAGAAGTTTCAGGGCCAAGTCATTTGCCAC, 42 nt into the exon from the acceptor, predicted 62.0% (PMC6838919, PMC5957359). Exon 55: H55A(-10+20) and Ex55_Ac0 (see above). All four are off-the-shelf starting sequences; no published oligo or cocktail has been reported as deliberately aimed at a del54-55 product.  
Target evidence: n/a — both exons canonical with intact splice sites. Protein-level validation is the gap: the UMD-DMD analysis lists '54 & 55' as a double-skip strategy but supplies no phenotype data for patients carrying that in-frame deletion (PMC11593839). I found no del54-55 Becker case report. Searches: web 'DMD in-frame deletion exons 54-55 Becker phenotype', 'dystrophin deletion exons 54-55 patient Becker mild phenotype Leiden DMD database'. Nearest proxies: ~90% of del45-55 patients (75/83) are BMD not DMD (PMC6313462), and a CRISPR Dmd del52-55 mouse made near-normal muscle/heart when young but was not protected against exercise damage at 52 weeks (PMC12519546).  
Coordinated: YES — the strongest finding in this report. Adams/Fletcher/Wilton: 'Targeting human or canine dystrophin exon 8 always leads to transcripts missing exons 8 and 9, whereas directing AOs to human exons 17, 34 or 54 induces transcripts missing the targeted exon as well as 17+18, 34+35 and 54+55 respectively' (PMC1933433). Independently restated by Mitrpant et al.: 'directing an AO to human dystrophin exon 54 led to removal of both exons 54 + 55', citing Wilton 2007 (PMC2835229, PMID 17285139). A web snippet of the primary source describes exon-54 targeting giving exon-54-only and exon-54+55 transcripts in roughly equal amounts; I could not open Wilton 2007 full text (Cell Press 403) to confirm that quantification, so treat the ratio as unverified. Caveat: the same patent that lists H54A(+67+97) describes it only as 'strong and consistent exon 54 skipping' with no mention of exon 55, so co-skipping may be oligo- or site-dependent and must be re-measured per candidate oligo.  
Linked: Brogidirsen: one PMO with two linked sequences to two sites in exon 44; mean dystrophin 20.55% of normal (24.47% in cohort 2) in Phase 1/2, 4.5-year extension data presented at MDA 2026 (PMID 40725431, NCT05996003). Yokota-lab cocktails routinely co-administer 2-11 separate PMOs (PMC6838919). Exon 48 needed two different PMOs to skip well in that screen — precedent that one exon may demand two arms.  
Amenability: Both exons are reachable at their 3' splice sites/early exonic ESEs; published oligos exist for each. Mechanistically exons 54 and 55 behave as a coupled splicing unit in human pre-mRNA (exon-54 targeting pulls exon 55 out with it), which is exactly what this block needs and makes it the most 'exon-definition-friendly' of the in-frame options. No endogenous 54+55 skipping was reported: the only systematic endogenous study found eight multi-exon-skipped species around the hotspot, the ideal exon44-56 (i.e. 45-55 skipped) species at ~600-fold lower abundance than full-length mRNA, and it could not resolve short/single skips (PMC5085753). So there is no meaningful baseline to build on — the event must be driven.

**Double skip of exons 55-56 (co-lead: 363 bp, -121 aa, 54|57 junction)**  
Published: Exon 55: H55A(-10+20) (US9758783B2) and Ex55_Ac0 PMO (PMC6838919). Exon 56: H56A(+92+121), reported to give strong and consistent exon 56 skipping (US9758783B2). Exon 56 is outside the 45-55 hotspot, so it is absent from the Yokota PMO cocktail tables — fewer validated starting sequences than for exon 54.  
Target evidence: n/a — canonical exons. Protein validation equally absent: UMD-DMD lists '55 & 56' at 0.6% of all mutations / 2.0% of deletions with no phenotype data for the in-frame del55-56 genotype (PMC11593839). Searches as above found no del55-56 Becker case report.  
Coordinated: None found. The published coupling reports in human DMD are 8+9, 17+18, 34+35 and 54+55 (PMC1933433); exon 55/56 is not among them, and nothing indicates an exon-55 oligo pulls exon 56 out or vice versa. Searches: PubMed 'dystrophin exon 54 exon 56 antisense oligonucleotide skipping efficiency' (0 hits), 'double exon skipping single antisense oligonucleotide dystrophin two adjacent exons' (0 hits); web '"exon 56" dystrophin antisense oligonucleotide skipping efficiency "exon 55" cocktail PMO', 'single antisense oligonucleotide induced skipping of two adjacent dystrophin exons'; Europe PMC down (503). Assume two oligos are required.  
Linked: Same precedent as above (brogidirsen dual-arm single molecule; multi-PMO cocktails). A two-arm linked oligo carrying an exon-55 and an exon-56 sequence is architecturally supported by brogidirsen but has no DMD multi-exon precedent.  
Amenability: Exon 55 acceptor-site targeting is documented; exon 56 has a working exonic-ESE oligo at +92+121. Neither exon shows reported endogenous skipping. Because exon 56 is not part of any coupled pair in the literature, 55-56 inherits the full multiplicative penalty of needing two independent skips in the same transcript, whereas 54-55 does not. That asymmetry, not the 6-aa difference in deleted protein, should decide the lead.

**Larger in-frame blocks containing exon 55 (52-55, 54-57, 50-55, 52-57, 53-58, 54-59, up to 45-55)**  
Published: Richest precedent of any candidate here. Yokota-lab mutation-tailored PMO cocktails for 45-55 include Ex53_Ac9 (GTTCTTGTACTTCATCCCACTGATTCTGAA, 73.9% predicted), Ex54_Ac42 and Ex55_Ac0 (PMC6838919, PMC5957359). In patient-derived myotubes, 45-55-skipped mRNA reached 61%, 43% and 27% with cocktails at 10 uM each PMO (PMC5957359). Bodywide 45-55 skipping was achieved in mdx52 mice with systemic antisense delivery (PMID 22869723), and in vivo single- and multi-exon skipping protocols in mdx52 are published (PMID 30171548, 40720023).  
Target evidence: Endogenous multi-exon skipping at this hotspot exists but is negligible: eight MES species detected, the exon44-56 (45-55-skipped) species ~600-fold less abundant than full-length DMD mRNA (~12 extra PCR cycles) (PMC5085753). Protein validation is best for the widest block: ~90% of del45-55 patients are BMD (75/83) (PMC6313462). For 52-55 specifically, a CRISPR Dmd del52-55 mouse had wild-type-like muscle and heart histology/function when young but the truncated protein was insufficient to protect against exercise-induced damage by 52 weeks (PMC12519546).  
Coordinated: Only the 54+55 coupling is documented (PMC1933433, PMC2835229). Beyond that, the field's own verdict is negative: 'a technical problem in AO-mediated multiple exon skipping is the difficulty in excluding all targeted exons from a pre-mRNA at the same time', and the authors advocate cocktails rather than single-oligo coordinated skipping (PMC6313462). A 52-55 block could in principle be 3 oligos rather than 4 if the 54-directed oligo carries exon 55 along.  
Linked: Cocktails of 2-11 PMOs are the established architecture (PMC6838919, PMID 22869723). DG9 cell-penetrating-peptide conjugation improved exon 51 skipping and muscle function in hDMDdel52;mdx mice, and a minimized 45-55 DG9-PMO mixture restored dystrophin locally — the only route found for making a many-oligo cocktail deliverable.  
Amenability: Individual exon amenability in 50-55 is good (published PMOs with measured >20% single-exon skipping at 5-10 uM); exon 53 was the weak member in one screen (only a faint band) and exon 48 needed two PMOs. Each added exon multiplies CMC, dosing and safety burden and deletes 110-485 aa more of the rod domain. Keep as fallback only if neither 54-55 nor 55-56 skips cleanly.

**Partial exon 55 removal via an exon-internal cryptic splice site**  
Published: None found against an exon-55 internal cryptic site. The nearest analogous published work is base-editor-mediated ESE disruption for exon skipping, which reports that exons with a strong 3' splice site and high ESE density tend to show cryptic-site activation instead of clean skipping — a warning, not a precedent.  
Target evidence: Not demonstrated. No report found of a cryptic acceptor or donor inside DMD exon 55, let alone one at a frame-compatible distance from the PTC. Searches: web 'DMD exon 55 splice site strength exonic splicing enhancer ... cryptic', 'exon 55 DMD nonsense mutation exon skipping strategy'; PubMed 'DMD exon 55 antisense oligonucleotide skipping'; Europe PMC unavailable (503). The one systematic analysis retrieved shows cryptic-site activation is a real competing outcome in DMD but is governed by splice-site strength and ESE density rather than being individually catalogued per exon, and it is the failure mode of splice-site-masking oligos, not a designable target.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: none found  
Amenability: Unassessable without a mapped cryptic site. Note the published exon-55 oligos (H55A(-10+20), Ex55_Ac0) both sit on the 3' splice site, which is precisely the configuration that can provoke cryptic acceptor use — so this outcome should be monitored as an off-target readout (the brief explicitly requires its absence) rather than pursued as a mechanism.

**Non-frame-restoring mechanisms: NMD evasion, output-raising/poison-exon block, uORF/3'UTR or transcriptional upregulation, allele knockdown**  
Published: None found for any of these mechanisms in DMD. Note also that no ASO mechanism induces translational readthrough of a PTC; that is small-molecule territory, so stabilising the exon-55 transcript yields only more rod-domain truncate lacking the cysteine-rich and C-terminal domains.  
Target evidence: No target element retrieved for any of these in DMD, and none would help: every one of them changes the amount, stability or translation of a message that still carries the exon-55 PTC. No DMD poison exon, repressive uORF or repressive natural antisense transcript was found in the searches run ('DMD exon 55 ... skipping', 'exon skipping for nonsense mutations in DMD', UMD-DMD applicability review, endogenous MES study). The endogenous-splicing survey of this locus reported only multi-exon-skipped and back-spliced circRNA by-products, not an NMD-coupled poison exon that throttles productive output (PMC5085753).  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: not applicable  
Amenability: Not applicable. Knockdown is actively harmful: in a hemizygous male there is no second allele to spare. These should be dropped from the program rather than carried as backups.

**Surrogate-gene upregulation by ASO (UTRN or modifier transcripts)**  
Published: none found in this retrieval  
Target evidence: No specific druggable repressive element in UTRN was located in the searches run here (the retrieval budget went to the splice candidates: searches were DMD/exon-skipping focused — 'DMD exon 55 ...', 'exon skipping applicability UMD-DMD', 'multiple exon skipping hot spots'; no dedicated UTRN uORF/3'UTR/antisense search was completed). Treat the UTRN element as unlocated, not as absent.  
Coordinated: not applicable  
Linked: not applicable  
Amenability: Unknown here. It is mutation-agnostic and therefore genuinely independent of the exon-55 PTC, but it does not address the lesion and its achievable fold-change in utrophin and mechanical benefit are not established by anything retrieved. Park it as a possible combination arm; do not let it compete for the lead.

Non-transferable benchmarks: Three numbers will be misread as expectations for this program. (1) Clinical single-skip dystrophin levels: brogidirsen mean 20.55% of normal (24.47% cohort 2) for exon 44 (PMID 40725431, NCT05996003) and DYNE-251 8.72% mean absolute muscle-adjusted dystrophin at 20 mg/kg Q4W (Dyne DELIVER release). Both are SINGLE-exon skips of exons with validated Becker genotypes, delivered by a dual-arm PMO or an Fc-TfR1 conjugate; they are chemistry, dosing, safety and delivery precedent only. A 54-55 or 55-56 product needs TWO exons out of the SAME transcript, so yield is multiplicative unless the 54+55 coupling does the work — the honest prior is a fraction of a single-skip number, not equal to it. (2) The 45-55 cocktail in vitro figures (61%/43%/27% skipped mRNA, PMC5957359) are transfection of immortalized or transdifferentiated patient cells with 10 uM of each of 5-6 PMOs — not a systemic-dose expectation, and measured as mRNA not protein. (3) Ex55_Ac0's '120.4%' and Ex54_Ac42's '62.0%' are outputs of the authors' predictive algorithm (PMC6838919), not measured skipping; the measured statement for those oligos is only '>20% at 5-10 uM'. Separately, the del45-55 Becker phenotype data (~90% BMD, 75/83) must not be transferred to del54-55 or del55-56: those two in-frame genotypes have NO retrieved patient phenotype data, and the del52-55 mouse shows a truncated rod-domain protein can look normal young yet fail under exercise stress at 52 weeks (PMC12519546).

Proposed designs:
- Lead: single PMO on exon 54 (H54A(+67+97) and Ex54_Ac42 as starting sequences) exploiting documented 54+55 coupling; one oligo, in-frame 53|56 junction.
- Lead backstop: two-PMO cocktail, exon-54 ESE oligo + exon-55 acceptor oligo (Ex55_Ac0 / H55A(-10+20)), to force the del54-55 product if coupling is partial.
- Parallel: dual-arm single molecule, brogidirsen-style, with linked exon-54 and exon-55 arms; one drug substance, two binding sites.
- Fallback: two-PMO exon 55 + exon 56 cocktail (Ex55_Ac0 + H56A(+92+121)) for the 54|57 in-frame junction if exon 54 skips poorly.
- Rescue: 3-4 PMO cocktail for the 52-55 block (Ex53_Ac9, Ex54_Ac42, Ex55_Ac0 + exon-52 oligo), DG9-peptide conjugated.

## Delivery platforms
**Unconjugated (naked) PMO — approved class plus dual-targeting next generation (brogidirsen)** — Phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomer, uncharged, no ligand. Uptake is non-receptor-mediated and favours leaky dystrophic fibres. Brogidirsen (NS-089/NCNP-02, Nippon Shinyaku) is a single PMO containing two connected sequences against two sites in the same exon — a payload-potency innovation, not a delivery one.  
Reach: Skeletal muscle myofibres: yes, but inefficiently; delivery is highest in actively degenerating/regenerating fibres. Heart: historically poor-to-absent — 'early in vivo mouse studies using systemic PMO have consistently shown poor or absent dystrophin restoration in the heart' (PMC6005479). Diaphragm reached less well than limb muscle. Satellite cells: not meaningfully. CNS: no (no BBB penetration after IV).  
Human PD: Eteplirsen (exon 51, 30 mg/kg/wk): ~0.4% of normal dystrophin at 48 wks, ~0.9% at 188 wks. Golodirsen (exon 53): 0.095%→1.019% at wk48. Casimersen (exon 45): 0.93%→1.74% at wk48. Viltolarsen (exon 53, 40 and 80 mg/kg/wk): 0.3%→5.7% and 0.6%→5.9% at wk25. Brogidirsen (exon 44, 40 and 80 mg/kg/wk, 24 wks): 10.27% and 15.79% of normal in one report, 16.63% and 24.47% in another; Phase 1/2 mean change +13.1% (p=0.03). No human cardiac dystrophin data for any of them.  
Dose: Weekly IV infusion, 30–80 mg/kg, indefinitely. Weekly infusion burden is a major real-world disadvantage versus Q4W/Q6W conjugates.  
Tox: Best-characterised safety record in the field; years of weekly dosing. Main issues are infusion reactions and renal-tubular monitoring (proteinuria, cystatin C); hypomagnesemia/eGFR decline seen with PPMOs is not a feature of naked PMO. Brogidirsen reported long-term safety with stable motor function.  
Access: Highest buildability. No delivery IP to license; PMO chemistry and CMC are commoditised with multiple CDMOs, and a 2-oligo cocktail for a 54+55 double skip is straightforward to manufacture and file as separate or combined drug substance. The dual-sequence connected design is Nippon Shinyaku IP and would need freedom-to-operate review if copied.

**Anti-TfR1 monoclonal antibody–PMO conjugate (Avidity AOC; del-zota / AOC 1044)** — Full IgG monoclonal antibody against transferrin receptor 1 (TfR1/CD71), non-cleavable linker, PMO cargo. TfR1 is highly expressed on skeletal and cardiac myocytes.  
Reach: Skeletal muscle: yes, clinically demonstrated by biopsy exon-skipping and dystrophin. Heart: demonstrated in animals only — mouse-active variant gave dose-dependent cardiac dystrophin (reported up to ~5% of wild-type in hDMD/exon-45-del mice) and NHPs showed dose-dependent PMO concentration and exon-44 skipping across muscles including heart. No human cardiac biopsy. CNS: not claimed for the muscle-optimised conjugate; TfR1 is separately an established BBB shuttle receptor, so CNS reach is a plausible but unproven extension, not something to assume.  
Human PD: EXPLORE44, 5 mg/kg × 3 doses Q6W, n=10 at 4 months: statistically significant +37% exon-44 skipping (up to 66% skipping observed) and +25% of normal dystrophin, mean dystrophin ~32% and up to 54% of normal; CK reduced >80% from baseline to near-normal.  
Dose: 5 mg/kg IV every 6 weeks — the lowest dose and least frequent schedule of any clinically active DMD exon-skipping agent.  
Tox: Favourable in EXPLORE44/OLE; most TEAEs mild or moderate. Class watch-items for TfR1 agents remain reticulocyte/erythroid effects and infusion reactions; retrieved sources report no reduction in receptor protein with repeat dosing for TfR1 ligands generally. No hypomagnesemia signal reported.  
Access: Effectively closed. Avidity was acquired by Novartis (~$12bn, agreed Oct 2025, closing H1 2026) specifically for the AOC delivery platform; del-zota's BLA was accepted for priority review under accelerated approval on 8 Sep 2026 with Phase 3 SAFARI44 as confirmatory. A new exon-54/55 asset on this platform would require a Novartis partnership, which is realistic only as an in-licence of our sequence into their platform, not the reverse.

**Anti-TfR1 Fab–PMO conjugate (Dyne FORCE; DYNE-251 / z-rostudirsen)** — Fab fragment against TfR1, cleavable linker, PMO cargo. Smaller than a full IgG, intended to improve tissue penetration and reduce Fc-mediated effects.  
Reach: Skeletal muscle: yes, biopsy-confirmed. Heart: preclinical only — FORCE-M23D gave robust skipping and durable dystrophin restoration in mdx mice, with the platform described as delivering to skeletal and cardiac muscle; no human cardiac dystrophin measurement retrieved. Respiratory: lung function reported preserved at 6 months (functional, not tissue, evidence). CNS: not claimed.  
Human PD: DELIVER: 20 mg/kg Q4W at 6 months, n=8 — 3.71% of normal dystrophin unadjusted, 8.72% muscle-content-adjusted (>10-fold the 0.3% reported for eteplirsen). Registrational Expansion Cohort primary endpoint met: 5.46% of normal muscle-content-adjusted, 7-fold from baseline, p<0.0001.  
Dose: 20 mg/kg IV every 4 weeks (10, 20 and 40 mg/kg cohorts studied).  
Tox: 54 participants, ~675 doses, >50 patient-years: most TEAEs mild or moderate; 2 serious adverse events in the 40 mg/kg group, both recovered — i.e. a dose ceiling is visible above 20 mg/kg.  
Access: Dyne is independent and the platform is its core asset; a partnership or cargo-in-licence for a new exon target is conceivable but Dyne is mid-registration (US accelerated-approval submission targeted Q2 2026) and will prioritise its own pipeline. Treat as partner-dependent, not internally buildable.

**Cell-penetrating peptide–PMO (PPMO): Sarepta SRP-5051/vesleteplirsen, PepGen EDO, preclinical Pip6a-PMO** — Cationic arginine-rich or related cell-penetrating peptide covalently fused to PMO; receptor-independent, charge-driven uptake and endosomal escape.  
Reach: Best preclinical cardiac reach of any PMO format: Pip6a-PMO gave 37.2% dystrophin in mdx and 51.6% in Cmah-/-mdx hearts with normalised cardiac output and restored ventricular function — proof that cardiomyocytes are reachable. In humans, however, the clinical PPMOs were limited by toxicity or insufficient exposure. CNS: no.  
Human PD: SRP-5051 30 mg/kg monthly, 12 weeks: 18× exon skipping and 8× dystrophin versus eteplirsen 30 mg/kg weekly × 24 weeks. PGN-EDO51 10 mg/kg: exon-51 skipped transcripts 4.26% but total dystrophin only 0.59% of normal.  
Dose: Monthly IV (SRP-5051 30 mg/kg Q4W; PGN-EDO51 up to 10 mg/kg Q4W).  
Tox: The reason this class failed. SRP-5051: hypomagnesemia, in some participants prolonged after stopping, plus eGFR decline; dosing stopped 6 Nov 2024 and the programme was discontinued on long-term safety/tolerability grounds. PGN-EDO51 was itself well tolerated (all related AEs mild, no SAEs) but was discontinued in May 2025 because PepGen could not dose high enough for meaningful dystrophin. Pip6a-PMO killed 5/15 mice at 18 mg/kg fortnightly, suggesting cumulative toxicity.  
Access: PepGen's EDO platform is explicitly open to partnering/licensing and the company has exited DMD, so the chemistry is obtainable cheaply — but it is obtainable because it did not work at tolerated doses in DMD. Sarepta's PPMO is deprioritised. Do not build the lead on this class; it is useful only as chemistry/dosing precedent and a cardiac-reach existence proof.

**Endosomal Escape Vehicle cyclic peptide–PMO (Entrada ENTR-601-44)** — Proprietary cyclic cell-penetrating peptide (EEV) conjugated to PMO, designed for endosomal escape rather than receptor targeting.  
Reach: Skeletal muscle: yes, with dose-dependent muscle drug exposure and skipping shown at WMS 2026. Heart and CNS: no human data retrieved.  
Human PD: ELEVATE-44-201 Cohort 1 (reported May 2026): +2.36% dystrophin over a 4.00% baseline and +2.31% exon skipping over a 2.66% baseline. Dose escalation continues; Cohort 3 goes up to 18 mg/kg with data expected after year-end 2026.  
Dose: IV, escalating cohorts up to 18 mg/kg; exact interval not specified in the sources retrieved.  
Tox: Met primary safety objectives in Cohort 1; a prior FDA clinical hold on the programme was removed. No hypomagnesemia-type signal retrieved.  
Access: Entrada is independent, mid-dose-escalation, and running parallel exon 44/45/50/51 programmes — partnering for a new exon target is possible but the platform's delivered dystrophin so far (~2–3 percentage points) is well below the TfR1 conjugates. Second-tier option.

**TfR1-binding bicyclic peptide–oligonucleotide conjugate (Ionis/Bicycle, BCY17901) — preclinical** — ~2 kDa synthetic bicyclic peptide ligand for TfR1 conjugated to ASO or siRNA. Keeps receptor targeting but is a fully synthetic small conjugate rather than a biologic, so CMC resembles oligo manufacture, not antibody manufacture.  
Reach: Skeletal muscle and heart in human-TfR1 knock-in mice and cynomolgus monkeys. Demonstrated with Dmpk/Malat1 ASOs and Hprt siRNA, not yet with a DMD exon-skipping cargo — and PMO cargo compatibility is not established in what was retrieved.  
Human PD: None found. No clinical data retrieved for this ligand class in muscle.  
Dose: Preclinical: 3.5 mg/kg IV or SC in mice; 25 mg/kg IV in NHP. A subcutaneous route is credible for this format, which no TfR1 antibody conjugate offers.  
Tox: No receptor protein depletion after repeated dosing; no off-target binding beyond TfR1 in high-throughput screening. No chronic-tox or human safety data.  
Access: Licensable in principle — Bicycle partners its ligands routinely and Ionis co-authored the work — but it is pre-IND for muscle and would need a PMO- or 2'-MOE-compatible linker built and validated for a splice-switching cargo. Realistic as a second-generation delivery bet behind a near-term lead, not as the first filing.

**Other receptor-targeted and nanoparticle approaches (satellite-cell aptamer-gold, integrin α7β1) — preclinical only** — Aptamer against α7/β1 integrin dimers on functionalised gold nanoparticles carrying nucleic-acid cargo; α7/β1 integrins are highly specific to muscle progenitors and myofibres and near-absent elsewhere.  
Reach: The only retrieved platform that explicitly targets muscle stem/satellite cells — the compartment that governs durability of correction in regenerating fibres, which is the one tissue the TfR1 conjugates do not address. Heart and CNS not addressed.  
Human PD: None found.  
Dose: Local or systemic in mice; no translatable regimen.  
Tox: Not characterised for chronic systemic gold-nanoparticle dosing in humans; this is a material-accumulation risk for a lifelong therapy.  
Access: Academic (Nature Communications 2024); would be an internal build or academic collaboration. Research-stage only — relevant to the satellite-cell durability question in the brief, not to a near-term filing.

Unconjugated baseline: Best current unconjugated baseline for DMD exon skipping is brogidirsen (NS-089/NCNP-02), an unconjugated dual-sequence PMO against exon 44: 40 or 80 mg/kg IV weekly for 24 weeks gave 10.27% and 15.79% of normal dystrophin in one report and 16.63% and 24.47% in another, with a Phase 1/2 mean change from baseline of +13.1% (p=0.03) and reported long-term safety with stable motor function (https://www.cell.com/cell-reports-medicine/fulltext/S2666-3791(24)00672-4; https://www.neurologylive.com/view/brogidirsen-demonstrates-long-term-safety-stable-motor-function-dmd). The approved-drug baseline is far lower and defines what a generic naked PMO achieves: eteplirsen 30 mg/kg/wk ~0.4% of normal at 48 weeks and ~0.9% at 188 weeks; golodirsen 0.095%→1.019% at week 48; casimersen 0.93%→1.74% at week 48; viltolarsen 80 mg/kg/wk 0.6%→5.9% at week 25. All require weekly lifelong IV infusion. None has human cardiac dystrophin data, and systemic naked PMO shows poor or absent cardiac restoration in animals — so for a programme whose stated tissue list includes cardiomyocytes and diaphragm, naked PMO leaves the heart essentially untreated.

Payload vs delivery: Delivery dominates the step from ~1% (eteplirsen/golodirsen/casimersen) to 5–9% (DYNE-251, same exon 51/53 biology, TfR1-conjugated). Target amenability and sequence potency dominate the exon-44 numbers: unconjugated brogidirsen reached 10–24% on exon 44, so del-zota's 25% is not a delivery benchmark transferable to a two-oligo exon 54+55 skip. Cardiac reach is purely delivery.
