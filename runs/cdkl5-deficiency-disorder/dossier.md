# Therapeutic development dossier: CDKL5 deficiency disorder

*Decision-support output from an automated multi-agent analysis. Claims are labelled EVIDENCE / INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS. Not medical advice; replaces no experimental validation.*

## 1. Disease mechanism

**Causal defect:** Loss-of-function variants in the X-linked gene CDKL5 (Xp22). CDKL5 makes a serine/threonine kinase. Variant types include deletions, truncating, splice and missense variants, most of them in the catalytic domain. Most patients are heterozygous females, who are mosaic because of X-inactivation; hemizygous males are usually more severely affected. Genotype groups (truncating vs missense; females vs males) may behave differently.

**Molecular consequence:** Patients lose CDKL5 kinase activity. Its known physiological substrates are microtubule-associated proteins, mainly MAP1S, EB2 and ARHGEF2 (target motif R-P-X-S/T-A/G/P/S), and these proteins become under-phosphorylated. Without MAP1S phosphorylation, MAP1S stays bound to microtubules. Dendritic microtubules then grow for longer, and cargo transport along dendrites is impaired. The downstream effects are reduced dendritic arborization, abnormal synapse formation and maturation, and altered Akt/rpS6 signalling. Proposed roles in phosphorylating MECP2 and in forming cilia are less well established.

**Key cell types:** Forebrain excitatory (glutamatergic) cortical and hippocampal neurons, Forebrain inhibitory (GABAergic) interneurons, Visual cortex circuits (linked to cortical visual impairment)

**Key pathology:**
- Seizures starting in early infancy that resist treatment (developmental and epileptic encephalopathy)
- Reduced dendritic arborization and abnormal maturation of synapses
- Faulty regulation of dendritic microtubule growth and cargo transport
- Cortical visual impairment (reduced visual evoked responses in mice)
- Low muscle tone (hypotonia), severe developmental delay, movement and sleep disorders

**Evidence strength:** Moderate. The cause (monogenic loss of function) is certain, and two independent studies have validated physiological substrates. The link from loss of substrate phosphorylation to seizures and to the patients' symptoms is not established.

**Major unknowns:**
- How losing microtubule substrate phosphorylation leads to epilepsy (knockout mice do not have spontaneous seizures; PMID:24838000)
- Whether symptoms can be reversed, and over what time window (not found in retrieved literature)
- Whether missense variants have residual or dominant-negative activity, and how the genotype groups differ
- How much X-inactivation mosaicism explains differences in severity between females
- Whether MECP2 phosphorylation and roles in cilia or the nucleus matter in the disease
- Which neuron subtypes and which developmental stage drive seizures vs cognitive problems

CDD is caused by loss-of-function variants in X-linked CDKL5, a serine/threonine kinase most abundant in brain (EVIDENCE, supportive: MONDO:0100039; UniProt:O76039). Two independent phosphoproteomic studies converge on microtubule regulators MAP1S, EB2 and ARHGEF2 as the physiological substrates; loss of MAP1S phosphorylation leaves MAP1S bound to dendritic microtubules, lengthening growth episodes and impairing dendritic cargo transport, with downstream reduced dendritic arborization, abnormal synapse maturation and altered Akt/rpS6 signalling (EVIDENCE, supportive: PMID:30266824; PMID:30377159). Lowering MAP1S rescues the microtubule-dynamics defect in Cdkl5-null neurons - the field's only clean rescue experiment (EVIDENCE, supportive: PMID:30266824). AAV-delivered CDKL5 raises phospho-EB2 in mouse brain, making pEB2 a quantitative pathway-activity biomarker (EVIDENCE, supportive: PMID:39033321). The decisive uncertainty is the causal chain from lost substrate phosphorylation to the clinical phenotype, above all the infantile pharmacoresistant epilepsy: Cdkl5 knockout mice have no spontaneous seizures, only altered convulsant sensitivity, reduced visual evoked potentials and reduced dendritic complexity (EVIDENCE, contradictory for seizure modelling: PMID:24838000). Human CDD patient-derived guided cortical organoid neurons do show increased firing rate and network synchrony with reduced pEB2, but the phenotype is protocol-dependent and absent in NGN2-induced neurons from the same donors (EVIDENCE, supportive-with-caveat: PMID:40930428). Whether any deficit is reversible after symptom onset is not established in the retrieved literature (HYPOTHESIS, not_studied).

## 2. Therapeutic leverage points

- Phospho-EB2 as a quantitative, in vivo-validated pathway-activity readout that any CDKL5-restoring agent must move, and that any downstream (MAP1S-lowering, excitability) agent must NOT move - giving every arm a built-in specificity control (EVIDENCE: PMID:39033321; PMID:40930428).
- MAP1S protein dose as the single node where a genuine rescue has been shown, and whose required perturbation (partial lowering) is exactly what RNase-H gapmer and RNAi pharmacology deliver (EVIDENCE: PMID:30266824).
- Visual evoked potential amplitude: a quantitative, translatable, non-seizure efficacy endpoint available today in Cdkl5-null mice and clinically meaningful given cortical visual impairment (EVIDENCE: PMID:24838000).
- Human CDD organoid MEA hyperexcitability with isogenic and NGN2 specificity controls - the only retrieved human-tissue functional network phenotype, usable to separate genotype-selective correction from generic firing suppression (EVIDENCE: PMID:40930428).
- A dose/duration/mosaic-fraction threshold experiment using transfected CDKL5 mRNA as a research tool, which gates every restoration modality at low cost (INFERENCE from PMID:39033321).
- Established intrathecal oligonucleotide dosing in paediatric neurology as a ready delivery route for any transcript-level intervention (EVIDENCE: PMID:34412058).

### Comparative judgment

Three hypotheses, but only one rests on a rescue experiment, and that asymmetry should drive allocation. MAP1S-brake release is the only arm where a published manipulation reversed a disease phenotype in disease-genotype cells (PMID:30266824), and its required perturbation - partial protein dose reduction - maps cleanly onto one modality class. Within that hypothesis the modalities are not interchangeable. The oligonucleotide gapmer wins over RNAi for the same target for a non-obvious reason: the arm's in vivo risk is a spatial gradient of knockdown, and because brake release overshoots rather than merely under-treating, a gradient can be actively harmful; bolus gapmer pharmacology makes regional PD easier to titrate and measure, which is what makes a negative result interpretable. Durable di-siRNA becomes the better chronic implementation only after the window and gradient questions are answered. The small-molecule version is rejected on two independent grounds (no pocket, no in vivo proof-of-concept) while keeping its one genuine advantage - uniform forebrain access - on the table for later. All three MAP1S modalities share a single cheap gate, the two-genotype graded knockdown matrix; if no window exists, no delivery chemistry saves any of them, so that experiment precedes modality-specific spend. CDKL5 output boost has the most certain biology - raising functional CDKL5 is unambiguously the right direction, with pEB2 as a quantitative success criterion (PMID:39033321) - and the weakest denominator. The modality comparison is stark: the splice-switching oligonucleotide has a real mechanism, route and n-of-few precedent (PMID:34412058) but may have almost no eligible patients; the small molecule has an agent already dosed in CDD (NCT02758626) but low expected readthrough efficiency and, for the chaperone branch, an unresolved direction of effect that could make the intervention harmful; the RNA implementation is rejected outright because the vehicle does not exist and its failure would be silent, with bulk pEB2 reading positive from ependyma while the driving neurons are untouched. The right move is to spend almost nothing on chemistry and buy two shared gating datasets: the in silico variant-class count (does a splice-correctable population exist?) and the variant-function catalogue with the dominant-negative co-expression test (which alleles are hypomorphic, and is stabilisation safe?). Both can kill expensive programmes for the cost of weeks. E/I rebalance inverts the pattern: best delivery, the only approved CDD drug, worst mechanistic specification. Its small-molecule arm is worth funding not to find another anti-seizure medicine - soticlestat's negative Phase 3 (NCT03635073) and CDD pharmacoresistance (MONDO:0100039) show that ceiling - but because the genotype x drug interaction design asks whether any excitability mechanism is CDD-selective, the question that distinguishes disease modification from symptom suppression. Its protein arm is the clearest reject: the perturbation wanted is continuous dendrite-level modulation of every forebrain neuron from infancy, exactly what biologics cannot deliver, and no surface receptor has been nominated; it survives only as a few extra wells of ligand probe inside the organoid experiment. Across hypotheses, the most under-appreciated cross-cutting gap is reversibility and timing. Nothing retrieved shows that restoring CDKL5 or correcting its downstream node after symptom onset improves anything, and CDD seizures begin in the first months of life. Every strategy here is bounded by that unknown, which is why the MAP1S in vivo study should be dosed post-symptomatically rather than neonatally - a design choice that buys window information at no extra cost. What would change my mind: a positive post-symptomatic MAP1S in vivo result with confirmed deep-cortex PD would move that arm to a lead-optimisation commitment and make the small-molecule tractability question worth asking; a confirmed-PD negative would retire MAP1S across all modalities and shift everything to CDKL5-level restoration and circuit work. Demonstration of a genuine non-productive splicing element in human neuronal CDKL5 transcripts would transform the splice-ASO arm from n-of-1 capability into a generalisable programme and likely make it the highest-value arm here. A genotype-selective interaction for any already-dosed excitability drug would justify a CDD-specific clinical hypothesis with VEP as a non-seizure pharmacodynamic endpoint. And a seizure-competent CDD model with spontaneous seizures would change the calculus for every arm, because at present no approach here can be tested against the endpoint families care about most.

### MAP1S-brake release - oligonucleotide - PURSUE

*Hypothesis:* The proximal consequence of CDKL5 loss is persistent, unphosphorylated MAP1S binding to dendritic microtubules, producing over-long microtubule growth episodes and impaired dendritic cargo transport. Lowering MAP1S dose (or otherwise reducing its microtubule-binding occupancy) should restore normal microtubule dynamics and, downstream, dendritic arborization and synapse maturation, without needing CDKL5 kinase activity.

*Target / process:* MAP1S (and more broadly the CDKL5-substrate node MAP1S/EB2/ARHGEF2 controlling dendritic microtubule dynamics and cargo transport); *desired perturbation:* Partial reduction of MAP1S protein level or of its microtubule association in forebrain neurons; normalization of microtubule growth-episode length and dendritic transport

*Strategy:* Gapmer RNase-H1 ASO against MAP1S mRNA, delivered intrathecally/intracerebroventricularly for broad CNS neuronal uptake, titrated to partial (~40-60%) knockdown rather than maximal suppression. MAP1S is an intracellular, cytoplasmic microtubule-binding protein, so protein-level or antibody approaches are excluded and transcript lowering is the natural way to execute the desired perturbation (reduce MAP1S dose). The first use is not a drug but a decisive in vivo tool: dose a MAP1S gapmer (or Malat1-style control) into Cdkl5-null mice and read dendritic arborization, visual evoked potentials and convulsant-threshold EEG. Because the intervention is downstream of the kinase, it is genotype-agnostic across deletions, truncating, splice and missense alleles.

*Verdict rationale:* The only arm where the dominant failure mode is directly attacked by the proposed experiment. The post-symptomatic ICV gapmer dose-response in Cdkl5-null and wild-type littermates, with mandatory regional MAP1S protein quantification paired to VEP, dendritic arbor and convulsant-threshold EEG, and pEB2 as specificity control, converts the field's one rescue result into an organism-level yes/no and simultaneously tests the postnatal window. Both outcomes are decision-grade: window-positive de-risks a genotype-agnostic target; negative with confirmed deep-cortex knockdown retires MAP1S for all modalities. PURSUE is earned by causal rescue evidence plus a credible, interpretable test - gated behind the in vitro two-genotype window matrix, because an absent window kills it before any chemistry.

*Delivery:* tissue CNS - cortex, hippocampus, visual cortex; cell Postmitotic forebrain neurons (excitatory and inhibitory); knockdown must occur in the neuronal soma to deplete MAP1S from dendritic microtubules; access Intrathecal/ICV gapmer ASOs are a clinically validated CNS delivery route with naked (unconjugated) uptake into neurons and glia; CSF dosing every 3-6 months is established practice in pediatric neurology. Free uptake into neurons is productive and RNase H1 is active in both nucleus and cytoplasm, so the relevant compartment is accessible for a cytoplasmic mRNA such as MAP1S.; constraint: Intrathecal ASOs distribute in a gradient: superficial cortex, spinal cord and brainstem are well covered, but deep structures and, critically, uniform deep-cortical/hippocampal neuronal knockdown is less complete; achieving the uniform partial lowering across forebrain excitatory and inhibitory neurons that this hypothesis requires - while staying inside an unknown therapeutic window - is the binding constraint. In infants with CDD (seizure onset in the first months), repeated intrathecal dosing from early infancy is procedurally feasible but adds risk.

*Biggest unknown:* Whether partial MAP1S lowering in vivo converts the microtubule-dynamics rescue into any organism-level benefit in Cdkl5-null mice, and whether that benefit survives after symptom onset (postnatal window). Secondarily, how much MAP1S lowering is tolerated by normal neurons.

*Model fitness (partial):* Cdkl5-null mouse (constitutive KO) dosed ICV with a Map1s RNase-H gapmer, read at the level of cortical/hippocampal dendritic morphology, convulsant-threshold EEG and visual evoked potentials (EVIDENCE: the KO's dendritic, EEG-to-convulsant, VEP and Akt/rpS6 phenotypes are published, PMID:24838000); primary Cdkl5-null neurons for the dose-ranging step (EVIDENCE: Map1s lowering rescued microtubule dynamics in exactly this preparation, PMID:30266824).. Mechanism: Yes for the proximal node: the KO lacks MAP1S/EB2/ARHGEF2 phosphorylation and shows the microtubule-dynamics defect that Map1s lowering corrects (EVIDENCE, PMID:30266824; PMID:30377159). ICV gapmer delivery reproduces the validated perturbation (dose reduction of MAP1S) rather than an analogue of it (INFERENCE). Cell type: Partial. Naked ICV/intrathecal gapmers reach forebrain excitatory and inhibitory neurons but on a ventricular-to-deep gradient, so knockdown depth will differ between the cell populations implicated in CDD behaviour (EVIDENCE that both excitatory and inhibitory forebrain neurons matter, PMID:24838000; HYPOTHESIS, not_studied, that a tolerated dose reaches enough of each). Phenotype: Quantitative: MAP1S protein per region (PD), dendritic arbor complexity, VEP amplitude, PTZ/convulsant EEG threshold. Phospho-EB2 serves only as a negative control here - MAP1S lowering acts downstream of the kinase and should NOT restore it (INFERENCE from PMID:39033321). Limits: Cdkl5-null mice have no spontaneous seizures, only abnormal convulsant responses, so the endpoint families care about most cannot be tested in this model (EVIDENCE, contradictory, PMID:24838000). Non-uniform knockdown means a negative in vivo result is ambiguous between 'wrong mechanism' and 'insufficient coverage' - which is why regional PD must be measured in the same animals.

*Best next experiment:* Single ICV bolus dose-response of a validated Map1s gapmer (3 doses) versus a control oligonucleotide in Cdkl5-null and wild-type littermates, dosed after phenotype onset (~P30-P45) rather than neonatally, so the result speaks to the post-symptomatic window.    - Supports if: At least one knockdown level makes null neurons indistinguishable from untreated wild-type on both microtubule and dendritic readouts while wild-type neurons at that same level are also unaffected - a defined window that becomes the design specification for all MAP1S modalities.
   - Weakens/kills if: Wild-type curves degrade at or below the depth that corrects null neurons (no window), or null dendritic arborization fails to improve at any depth despite corrected microtubule dynamics. Either outcome stops MAP1S lowering across oligonucleotide, RNA and small-molecule arms - no delivery chemistry rescues a mechanism with no window.
2. **In silico CDKL5 variant-class census: classify all reported CDKL5 variants in public variant and registry datasets into deletion / frameshift / nonsense / missense / splice-region / deep-intronic, flagging splice-class alleles predicted to create cryptic sites or pseudoexons with retained partial normal splicing; output a count of candidate splice-correctable individuals against a pre-set go/no-go threshold. In parallel, a bounded long-read/RNA-seq check for a non-productive splicing element in human neuronal CDKL5 transcripts.**
   - Answers: Does a denominator exist for a CDKL5 splice-switching ASO, and is there a poison-exon element that would make an output-boost ASO generalisable instead of bespoke?
   - Requires: Database analysis, essentially no bench cost; plus one long-read sequencing experiment on human neuronal CDKL5 transcripts.
   - Supports if: A splice-correctable cohort of meaningful size (tens, not single digits) is identified, and/or a non-productive CDKL5 splicing element is demonstrated - the latter converting this into a generalisable programme serving all patients with one intact allele.
   - Weakens/kills if: The splice-correctable set is a handful of individuals worldwide and no non-productive element exists. Then stop the generalisable splice-ASO programme and retain the tiling protocol only as a dormant, on-demand n-of-1 capability triggered by a qualifying patient plus a sponsor.
3. **Variant-function catalogue with dominant-negative test: express patient kinase-domain missense alleles in CDKL5-null neurons and measure phospho-EB2 to classify each as null or hypomorphic; then co-express each with wild-type CDKL5 and test whether it suppresses wild-type-driven pEB2. For hypomorphic non-suppressing alleles, distinguish destabilised from catalytically dead (cycloheximide chase plus activity-per-unit-protein). Run alongside a dose/duration/mosaic-fraction titration using chemically modified CDKL5 mRNA as a tool reagent (graded dose versus pEB2 and dendritic arbor; single versus repeated pulse; mixed transfected/untransfected co-cultures at 10/25/50/75%).**
   - Answers: Is stabilising mutant CDKL5 safe or harmful, and how much CDKL5 protein, in what fraction of neurons, for how long, is enough? This is the shared threshold that makes every restoration result interpretable.
   - Requires: Cdkl5-null and patient-derived iPSC cortical neurons, mRNA/plasmid expression, quantitative pEB2 immunoassay, MEA for network readouts. No new chemistry.
   - Supports if: A meaningful set of alleles is hypomorphic, destabilised and non-suppressing (legitimate chaperone targets), and a clear restoration threshold emerges - e.g. partial restoration in a minority of neurons normalises pEB2 and dendritic metrics, with benefit persisting beyond the protein's presence. This lowers the bar for every restoration modality and justifies the in vivo timing study.
   - Weakens/kills if: Missense alleles are predominantly catalytically dead or dominant-negative - then do not start chaperone chemistry at all. Or phenotypes require near-complete, continuous CDKL5 in nearly all neurons with strictly cell-autonomous correction - then abandon transient-expression modalities and down-weight anything that cannot achieve broad sustained pan-neuronal correction.
4. **Post-symptomatic ICV gapmer study (gated on action 1 showing a window): single ICV bolus dose-response of a validated Map1s gapmer (3 doses) versus control oligonucleotide in Cdkl5-null and wild-type littermates, dosed at ~P30-P45 rather than neonatally. Four weeks later, in the same animals: regional MAP1S protein in superficial cortex, deep cortex and hippocampus (mandatory); phospho-EB2 as specificity control; VEP amplitude; convulsant-threshold EEG; sparse-label dendritic arbor quantification in visual and somatosensory cortex.**
   - Answers: Does partial MAP1S lowering convert the microtubule-dynamics rescue into organism-level benefit, and does it still work after phenotype onset (the postnatal window question that bounds all approaches)?
   - Requires: Gapmer synthesis and validation, ICV dosing, regional PD immunoassay, electrophysiology (VEP, convulsant EEG), histology. The regional PD measurement is non-negotiable - without it a negative result is uninterpretable.
   - Supports if: In regions where MAP1S is lowered 30-60%, VEP amplitude and/or dendritic complexity shift significantly toward wild-type with a dose-response, wild-type animals at the same exposure are unchanged, and pEB2 stays low - confirming the effect is downstream of the kinase rather than off-target CDKL5 restoration. Commit to lead optimisation and chronic tolerability.
   - Weakens/kills if: Verified 40-60% MAP1S lowering in deep cortex and hippocampus with no movement in VEP, dendritic arbor or convulsant threshold - or benefit only at doses that degrade wild-type animals. Stop the MAP1S arm for all modalities. If deep PD is poor, the question is delivery not biology: repeat with conditional genetic Map1s reduction before further chemistry spend.
5. **Genotype x drug organoid MEA study: record CDD patient-derived guided cortical organoid neurons versus isogenic controls across the early window where hyperexcitability is present, testing concentration-response panels of the clinically precedented CDD mechanisms (ganaxolone, fenfluramine, bexicaserin, soticlestat) plus an Akt/rpS6 probe in each direction, plus a few wells of extracellular ligands (IGF-1, IGF-1-derived peptide, BDNF, control growth factor) as the biologic target-nomination probe. Primary analysis is the genotype x drug interaction on firing rate and synchrony, not the main effect of drug; measure pEB2 and phospho-rpS6 at endpoint; run the identical panel in NGN2 neurons from the same donors as a phenotype-null specificity arm.**
   - Answers: Is any excitability or growth-factor mechanism CDD-selective - correcting mutant networks toward control - or does it merely dampen firing equally in mutant and control neurons? This separates disease modification from the demonstrated symptomatic ceiling and pre-registers the only condition under which a CDD biologic should be opened.
   - Requires: CDD and isogenic organoid cultures plus donor-matched NGN2 neurons, MEA recording, concentration-response panels, pEB2/phospho-rpS6 immunoassay. Marginal extra cost for the ligand probe arm.
   - Supports if: At least one mechanism produces significant genotype-selective normalisation - mutant firing/synchrony returns toward control at a concentration leaving control networks largely unaffected, absent in the NGN2 arm, ideally with phospho-rpS6 correction - and on follow-up the same agent raises convulsant threshold and increases VEP amplitude in Cdkl5-null mice. For an already-dosed drug this justifies a CDD-specific clinical hypothesis with VEP as a non-seizure pharmacodynamic endpoint.
   - Weakens/kills if: All agents suppress firing similarly in mutant and control networks (main effect, no interaction), or correct firing in vitro with no VEP improvement in vivo. Retire mechanism-blind repurposing here and redirect spend to identifying the neuron subtype and developmental window generating the epileptic phenotype and to building a seizure-competent CDD model. Either outcome also validates or kills the organoid MEA platform as the field's screening assay.

**Do not fund yet:**
- Any MAP1S-directed small-molecule or degrader hit-finding. MAP1S has no reported binding site, a degrader needs a binder first, and ChEMBL holds no curated mechanism even for CDKL5 (EVIDENCE: mcp__rp__chembl_target_drugs (CDKL5)). The target is simultaneously unligandable and un-de-risked in vivo; chemistry must stay gated behind the genetic window matrix and the ICV gapmer result.
- The non-selective brain-penetrant microtubule-stabiliser route (epothilone-class) for this node. No CDD-context data was retrieved and the mechanism cannot deliver selective MAP1S occupancy reduction (EVIDENCE, contradictory: none retrieved).
- CNS siRNA/di-siRNA chemistry and delivery development for MAP1S before the in vitro window is defined and before the in vivo gradient question is answered with a gapmer, where regional PD is easier to titrate. If no window exists, no delivery chemistry helps (INFERENCE from PMID:30266824).
- Any CDKL5 mRNA/LNP therapeutic programme. No neuron-tropic LNP exists at clinical maturity, CSF-delivered LNPs favour ependyma and meninges over deep neurons, and mRNA's days-long protein supply is mismatched to a lifelong developmental requirement. The specific hazard is silent failure in which bulk-tissue pEB2 reads positive from non-neuronal cells (EVIDENCE/INFERENCE, contradictory: none retrieved). Fund the mRNA only as a dose/duration/mosaic tool reagent.
- A CDKL5 poison-exon ASO programme. Its essential target - a non-productive splicing element in CDKL5 - was not established in retrieved evidence and must be demonstrated by long-read/RNA-seq first (EVIDENCE, contradictory: none retrieved).
- Bespoke per-patient splice-ASO toxicology packages before the variant-class census shows a cohort above a pre-set threshold. The dominant failure mode here is denominator, not chemistry (INFERENCE, contradictory: MONDO:0100039).
- A CDKL5 chaperone/stabiliser screening campaign before the dominant-negative co-expression test. If kinase-domain missense alleles are dominant-negative, stabilisation would increase a harmful species (HYPOTHESIS, not_studied).
- A renewed clinical readthrough attempt in CDD. Ataluren already completed Phase 2 in CDKL5 nonsense variants without producing a therapy, and its neuronal CNS exposure and readthrough efficiency are unknown; neuronal pEB2 data against a pre-specified threshold must come first (EVIDENCE, contradictory: NCT02758626).
- Any biologic programme for CDD, including BBB-shuttle engineering. No cell-surface node has been nominated, the chain from substrate hypophosphorylation to epilepsy is unestablished, and the perturbation wanted is the hardest biologic delivery problem there is (EVIDENCE, inconclusive: PMID:30266824; INFERENCE, contradictory). Allow only the few-well ligand probe inside the organoid experiment.
- Another Phase 2 signal-seeking trial of an existing excitability mechanism in CDD. Seizures are pharmacoresistant by definition and soticlestat's negative Phase 3 shows the ceiling; fund the genotype x drug interaction test instead (INFERENCE: MONDO:0100039; EVIDENCE: NCT03635073).
- Any programme whose value depends on post-symptomatic reversibility, until the timing question is addressed - which is why the in vivo MAP1S study should be dosed post-symptomatically rather than neonatally (HYPOTHESIS, not_studied).
